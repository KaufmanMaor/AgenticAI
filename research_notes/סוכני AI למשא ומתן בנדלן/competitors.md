# Competitive Landscape: AI agent-to-agent negotiation for residential real estate

**Research date:** 2026-11-20. **Method:** 24 web searches plus 1 successful page fetch (anthropic.com). Fetches to housingwire.com and businesswire.com were blocked by the session network, so those sources are cited from search-result snippets, not full text. **Scope:** US only. The Israel section was dropped at the coordinator's request.
**Verification convention:** a bullet with a link is *verified*, meaning a source exists and says this. Company-reported metrics are labelled "(company claim)". A bullet tagged **[unverified]** has no solid source.

---

## 1. AI real-estate brokerages and offer/negotiation platforms (US)

**Takeaway:** The closest US competitors either let **AI draft offers and run the workflow while licensed humans negotiate** (Homa, Ridley, TurboHome, HomeBuyMe) or **structure the offer process itself** (Indigo, Final Offer). We found **no company that runs autonomous agent-to-agent negotiation of price, repairs and terms on both sides**. The most direct threat is Homa, which is already live in Austin with a ~2% buyer rebate. That sets a reference price for the consumer: AI plus a rebate, not AI plus a fee.

**Cited Findings**
- **Homa**: an AI-native licensed buyer brokerage started by a former Zillow executive. Its seed round was reported at $5M, led by Quiet Capital ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/homa-raises-5m-expand-ai-homebuying-platform/), [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/business/homa-raises-5-million-to-redefine-homebuying-with-ai)).
  - It announced what it called the "first end-to-end AI-powered home purchases in the US" in December 2025 in Florida ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20251201257764/en/Homa-Announces-First-End-to-End-AI-Powered-Home-Purchases-in-the-United-States)).
  - It launched in Texas (Austin, Dallas, Houston, San Antonio) in June 2026 ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260615851906/en/), [Dallas Innovates](https://dallasinnovates.com/led-by-former-zillow-exec-home-buying-platform-homa-expands-to-texas/)). Axios reported that its first Texas deal was a $324K Austin home with a 2% rebate ([Axios](https://www.axios.com/local/san-antonio/2026/06/30/homa-texas-ai-real-estate-home-buying)).
  - Model: Homa keeps about 1% and credits about 2% to the buyer (company claim) ([The Close](https://theclose.com/news-homa-texas-buyer-rebates/)). Coverage says **licensed agents still negotiate the offers** ([News4Jax](https://www.news4jax.com/news/local/2026/07/16/ai-powered-real-estate-services-promise-homebuyers-thousands-in-savings-but-experts-warn-against-replacing-agents/)).
- **Indigo**: the former Ribbon team, branded as "the AI offer negotiation platform". It raised an $8M seed in January 2025 ([Inman](https://www.inman.com/2025/01/23/ai-home-transaction-platform-indigo-secures-8m-seed-round/), [Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/indigo-ai-based-home-negotiation-company-raises-8-million-seed)).
  - Listing "Storefronts" publish seller preferences, Contracts AI drafts and validates buyers' offers, and offers are compared side by side in shared deal rooms ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/real-estate/2024/indigo-begins-rollout-of-ai-powered-platform-for-buying-selling-homes/), [Inman 2024](https://inman.com/2024/08/15/former-ribbon-team-launches-ai-fueled-home-transaction-platform)).

  - It is the **closest in positioning** (transparency about preferences on both sides), but it is a tool for agents, not autonomous agents.
- **Final Offer**: a transparent-offer platform with a "buy it now" price, reverse offers and live counteroffers. It raised a ~$5M Series A from real estate agent investors ([Banker & Tradesman](https://bankerandtradesman.com/local-real-estate-tech-startup-final-offer-closes-series-a-funding-round), [Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/final-offer-5-million-funding/)).
  - **It launched in Texas with Keller Williams brokerages** ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/final-offer-launches-in-texas-with-keller-williams-brokerages/)).
  - Reported funding totals conflict across aggregators.
- **Ridley**: an AI seller platform in the FSBO style, launched in Colorado. It raised a $6.4M seed led by Fifth Wall in December 2025 ([Pulse2](https://pulse2.com/ridley-6-4-million-seed-funding/), [Inman](https://www.inman.com/2025/12/03/ridley-founder-says-platforms-not-agents-will-be-at-the-center-of-the-transaction/)).
  - Its offer analysis "simulates negotiation outcomes". Human "Pro" agents provide negotiation support for flat fees ([HousingWire](https://www.housingwire.com/articles/ridley-bets-big-that-real-estates-future-belongs-to-consumers-not-commissions/)).

  - **reAlpha**, which owns Prevu, entered Texas ([Real Estate News](https://www.realestatenews.com/2025/06/13/tech-bytes-realpha-enters-a-new-market-eyes-national-expansion)).

- **Agent-assist AI, not negotiation:**
  - HouseWhisper, nearly $10M seed ([VCA](https://www.vcaonline.com/news/2025022506/former-zillow-executives-launch-ai-powered-real-estate-agent-assistant-housewhisper-company-raises-nearly-10m-to-transform-agent-productivity/)).

## 2. General AI negotiation companies (B2B and consumer)

**Takeaway:** Autonomous AI negotiation **works in production for narrow, high-volume, low-stakes deals with multiple terms**. Pactum at Walmart is the canonical case: modest savings of ~1.5-3% and a deal rate of ~64-68%. Consumer "negotiate for you" services do best when the counterparty is a **standardized institution** (cable companies, car dealers), and they charge either success fees (Rocket Money at 30-60% of first-year savings) or flat fees (CarEdge at ~$40-79). DoNotPay shows the regulatory risk of overclaiming.

**Cited Findings**
- **Pactum**: an agentic procurement negotiation platform. It raised a $54M Series C led by Insight Partners in June 2025, bringing total funding past $100M. It reports 50+ enterprise clients and a multi-agent R&D focus ([Pactum](https://pactum.com/news-pactum-secures-54-million-in-series-c-funding-to-scale-agentic-ai-in-procurement/)).
  - At Walmart it closed deals with ~64% of suppliers at ~1.5% average savings, per Harvard PON as cited in coverage. Supplier preference for negotiating with the AI is reported at 75-85% (company and press figures) ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2023/walmart-finds-75-percent-vendors-prefer-negotiating-with-chatbot/partial/), [Fox Business](https://foxbusiness.com/technology/walmart-using-ai-negotiate-cost-purchase-terms-vendors-shorter-timeframe), [Pactum clients](https://pactum.com/clients)).
  - Deployments focus on smaller tail-spend suppliers.
- **Fairmarkit**: autonomous sourcing for tail spend. It raised a $35.6M Series C in 2022 (total $78M) and claims ~10% savings (company claim) ([Insight Partners](https://www.insightpartners.com/ideas/fairmarkit-secures-35-6-million-series-c-funding-to-help-more-enterprises-optimize-tail-spend/), [Fairmarkit](https://fairmarkit.com/autonomous-sourcing)).
- **Nibble**: a haggling chatbot that lets shoppers negotiate with online stores. It runs on Shopify, Magento, WooCommerce and an API, with merchant-set acceptance bounds. Pricing figures conflict: $499/month or 2% of attributed sales ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/07/16/negotiating-chatbot-nibble-ai-ecommerce/), [Shopify app](https://apps.shopify.com/nibble-chat-bot?locale=ko)).
- **CarEdge AI Negotiator**: an AI agent that emails and texts car dealers under an alias. Buyers can review its approach or give it full autonomy. It claims more than $1,000 and ~5 hours saved on average (company claim). Its price is listed at ~$40-79 in beta, with a human concierge at $999 ([CarEdge](https://caredge.com/ai-buying-agent), [6abc](https://6abc.com/post/ai-agent-helps-negotiate-car-deals-saving-buyers-time-money/17849776/), [WTOP](https://wtop.com/local/2025/10/would-you-let-ai-negotiate-a-car-deal-for-you)). CarWhere offers a competing service at $34.99/month ([CarWhere](https://www.carwhere.com/ai-car-negotiator)).
- **Rocket Money bill negotiation**: charges a success fee of 30-60% of first-year savings ([Rocket Money help](https://help.rocketmoney.com/en/articles/9744474-bill-negotiation-charge), [CNBC Select](https://www.cnbc.com/select/best-bill-negotiation-services/)).
- **DoNotPay**: the FTC settlement cost it $193K, and it is barred from claiming its product substitutes for professionals without evidence ([ABA Journal](https://www.abajournal.com/news/article/robot-lawyer-website-donotpay-settles-ftc-claims-it-couldnt-deliver-on-promises), [Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/ai/donotpay-robot-lawyer-fined-193k-by-the-ftc-for-not-being-a-lawyer-223227153.html?src=rss)).
- **AI salary negotiation**: a fragmented indie field of coaching and rehearsal tools with no clear leader, such as PayBoost and NegoWiz ([Product Hunt NegoWiz](https://www.producthunt.com/products/negowiz/launches), [App Store PayBoost](https://apps.apple.com/in/app/id6763983947)). These tools coach the user; they do not negotiate on the user's behalf.

## 3. Agent-to-agent commerce protocols and experiments

**Takeaway:** The infrastructure for agents transacting with agents is being standardized: Google's A2A and AP2 with signed "mandates", and OpenAI and Stripe's ACP. These protocols target checkout and payments, not real-estate contracts. Anthropic's **Project Deal** is the most relevant evidence. Agent-to-agent negotiation on behalf of real people worked (186 deals), but **the stronger model won better prices and the losing side didn't notice**. That is a central fairness and trust problem for any two-sided agent product.

**Cited Findings**
- **Anthropic Project Deal (April 2026):** 69 employees, each with $100, had Claude agents negotiate a Slack classifieds market. The result was 186 deals, just over $4,000 in total, with no human intervention.
  - Opus agents got ~$2.68 more per item as sellers and paid ~$2.45 less as buyers than Haiku agents. The same broken bike sold for $38 through Haiku and $65 through Opus.
  - Satisfaction and fairness ratings did not differ by model. Negotiation-style instructions had no significant effect. 46% of participants said they would pay for such a service.
  - Anthropic says the setup doesn't reflect how agents should be deployed in the real world ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/project-deal), [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/anthropic-says-stronger-ai-models-cut-better-deals-and-the-losers-dont-even-notice/)).
  - Legal commentary: the frameworks for agent mandates don't exist yet ([Legal Technology](https://legaltechnology.com/anthropics-ai-agent-to-agent-marketplace-experiment-the-legal-frameworks-dont-exist/)).
- **Google AP2**: an open payments protocol that extends A2A, using signed Mandates for authorization. It has 60+ partners ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/googles-new-agent-payments-protocol-ap2-allows-ai-agents-to-complete), [AP2 spec](https://ap2-protocol.org/index.md)).
- **OpenAI and Stripe ACP**: launched September 29, 2025 to power ChatGPT Instant Checkout ([agilebrandguide](https://agilebrandguide.com/wiki/agentic-ai/agentic-commerce-protocols/)). A claim that Instant Checkout has since been retired is **[unverified]**.
- **Academic evidence on LLM negotiators:**
  - "Warm" agents close more deals and "dominant" agents claim more value ([arXiv 2503.06416](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.06416v1)).
  - LLMs anchor at the extremes of the agreement zone and adapt less than humans ([arXiv 2512.13063](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.13063v1)).
  - Accurately modeling the counterparty does not by itself produce good strategy ([alphaXiv 2605.16575](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2605.16575)).
  - Several agents matched or beat MBA students on a benchmark ([arXiv 2602.05302](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.05302)).
  - These are abstract-level readings of preprints only.

## 5. Big incumbents' AI moves

*(Section numbering follows the original brief. Section 4, Israel, is out of scope.)*

**Takeaway:** Every portal and large brokerage shipped AI in 2025-26, but **all of it is search, insights, or productivity for agents**. Most explicitly keep "negotiation" as the human agent's job: Zillow says what remains for agents is "negotiation, strategy, trust and relationships". Portals are moving into chat surfaces such as the ChatGPT apps, which could become distribution channels or gatekeepers for any consumer agent.

## Inferences
1. **The white space is real but narrow.** No US product does *autonomous, two-sided, agent-to-agent* negotiation across multiple issues (price, repairs, credits, closing date, contingencies). The pieces already exist:
   - AI drafting offers: Homa, HomeBuyMe, Indigo Contracts AI.
   - Exposing preferences to the other side: Indigo Storefronts, Final Offer.
   - Pricing repairs: TheQwikFix, HENRY.
   - Autonomous negotiation elsewhere: Pactum, CarEdge.
   An incumbent or Homa could assemble them quickly.
2. **The founder's "both sides have agents" premise has a cold-start problem.** In practice the counterparty will be a human listing agent for years. A wedge that is realistic now is **one-sided**: an AI that negotiates with human listing agents, CarEdge-style, or an AI copilot for the buyer's agent. Agent-to-agent negotiation can follow if listing-side tools (Indigo, Final Offer) adopt a protocol.
3. **Licensing is a gating factor in Texas.** Negotiating on behalf of others for compensation is almost certainly brokerage activity. Every serious entrant (Homa, Ridley, TurboHome, reAlpha) is a licensed brokerage or uses licensed agents. Expect to need a sponsoring broker, and AI-only marketing invites scrutiny like the DoNotPay case.
4. **Economics benchmark:** The consumer reference point in Austin is Homa's ~2% rebate, about $10K on a $500K home. A negotiation product has to show integrative value (repairs, credits) on top of that. Selling it as a SaaS add-on to rebate brokerages or agents may beat a direct-to-consumer launch.
5. **The fairness asymmetry in Project Deal is a design opportunity and a liability.** If both sides use the same vendor, who does the agent serve? A neutral protocol with audited "mandates" (AP2-style), or the same model on both sides, could become a trust feature.
6. **Integrative win-win claims are plausible but unproven.** Research shows LLMs anchor at extremes and model counterparties without converting that into strategy. The founder's "super-smart win-win" claim needs benchmarking on real-estate scenarios.
