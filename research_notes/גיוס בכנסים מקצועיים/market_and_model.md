# Market and Business-Model Facts: On-Site Job Matching at Professional Conferences

(Research date: 2026-10-13. Most data is 2024-2026. Dates are noted where known. Many figures come from secondary or vendor sources, flagged as such.)

## 1. Market size: how many conferences, trade shows and business events, and how many attendees?

### Takeaway
The best global benchmark is the EIC / Oxford Economics study (2026 edition, 2025 data): **1.65 billion participant-visits** to business events and **US$1.3T direct spend**. No authoritative source gives a global **count of events**, so "millions of conferences" cannot be verified. It is only plausible under EIC's very broad definition (any business gathering of 10 or more people lasting 4 or more hours in a contracted venue), not for sizable professional conferences with recruiting potential.

### Cited Findings
- EIC 2026 Global Economic Significance of Business Events (with Oxford Economics, 2025 data): 1.65 billion participants across 180+ countries; US$1.3T direct spending; US$3.1T total business sales; US$1.8T GDP; 24.2M jobs supported (9.7M direct) — [IAVM summary](https://blog.iavm.org/eic-releases-2026-global-economic-significance-of-business-events-executive-summary/); [EIC news](https://news.eventscouncil.org/eic-releases-full-2026-global-economic-significance-of-business-events-study/)
- Trade shows alone drew **318 million participants** in 2025 — [IAVM summary](https://blog.iavm.org/eic-releases-2026-global-economic-significance-of-business-events-executive-summary/)
- Direct spend by segment (2025): corporate/business events $566B (43.8%); conventions, conferences and congresses **$383.2B**; trade shows $178.5B; incentives $86.6B — [Smart Meetings](https://www.smartmeetings.com/magazine_article/1-3-trillion-business-events-economic-impact); [C&MW](https://www.c-mw.net/new-eic-study-finds-business-events-contribute-us1-8-trillion-to-global-gdp/)
- EIC definition: a gathering of at least 10 people, lasting at least 4 hours, in a contracted venue. Social, educational and recreational events are excluded. The participant count is attendances, not unique people (the same person can be counted many times) — [Exhibitor Online](https://www.exhibitoronline.com/news/article.asp?ID=25306); [ALHI](https://www.alhi.com/blog/post/new-eic-research-reports-13-trillion-in-direct-spending-for-business-events/)
- Direct spend was up 12.2% over 2019. Oxford Economics forecasts $1.6T by 2028, about 6.7% a year — [C&MW](https://www.c-mw.net/new-eic-study-finds-business-events-contribute-us1-8-trillion-to-global-gdp/)
- Demand context: Q4 2024 RFP activity was at 98% of pre-pandemic levels (EIC Global Events Barometer) — [BizBash](https://www.bizbash.com/corporate-events/global-business-events-roaring-back-q4-rfp-activity-nears-pre-pandemic-peak-at-98-)
- UFI Global Barometer (36th edition, Dec 2025, 378 companies in 57 countries): 47% of exhibition organizers reported activity growth above 5% in 2025; 87% use AI in some form. The barometer measures sentiment, not event counts — [TravelDailyNews](https://www.traveldailynews.com/statistics-trends/ufi-global-barometer-indicates-activity-growth-in-several-markets-and-regular-use-of-ai-tools/)
- UFI publishes a separate Global Exhibition Industry Statistics report (May 2025 PDF) — [UFI](https://www.ufi.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/UFI_Global_Statistics_2025.pdf) (the figures could not be extracted; see Gaps)

### Inferences
- "Millions of conferences": I found no source for this. If it counts every corporate meeting of 10+ people, it may be roughly correct but irrelevant. The relevant market is **professional conferences and trade shows with hundreds to thousands of attendees and industry-specific talent**. That is likely in the tens of thousands of events per year globally, not millions (estimate, unverified).
- In a pitch, use participant and spend figures (1.65B attendances, 318M trade-show participants, $383B conference spend) rather than an event count.

### Gaps
- No authoritative global count of conferences or trade shows was found. The UFI statistics PDF and the full EIC report likely contain venue-space and exhibition counts but could not be retrieved. AMEX GBT Meetings & Events Forecast and Allied Market Research figures were not retrieved.

## 2. What share of attendees attend partly for jobs or career networking? (Founder claim: ~60%)

### Takeaway
**No survey found supports "~60% of conference attendees come partly to find jobs."** Surveys show **networking** is the top or second motive (about 40-52%). Professional development is about 53%. Explicit job-seeking is not measured as a motive in any event-industry survey found. Networking does matter heavily for hiring, but the widely cited "85% of jobs via networking" is poorly sourced.

### Cited Findings
- MPI survey: networking is the top reason for attending an event (52%) and is rated highly important (67%). For professional events, 53% cite professional development and 45% industry trends — [MPI](https://mpi.org/blog/article/the-key-to-understanding-event-attendees)
- Medical conferences (Ram et al. 2023, n=1,229): motivations cluster into learning, personal and social factors. 28.1% were motivated mainly by learning — [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10148698/)
- Librarians (n=794): 56% attend mainly for "professional rejuvenation" and 40% highly value networking — [EBLIP](https://journals.library.ualberta.ca/eblip/index.php/EBLIP/article/view/6293)
- "85% of jobs are filled via networking" is widely repeated but traced to Zippia or loose LinkedIn/HubSpot attributions, with no primary study — [Novoresume](https://novoresume.com/career-blog/networking-statistics); [WebProNews](https://www.webpronews.com/good-news-for-linkedin-85-of-jobs-filled-via-networking/)
- Referrals: about 7% of applicants but about 40% of hires (Zippia, secondary) — [Zippia](https://www.zippia.com/advice/networking-statistics/)
- Kickresume survey of hires (contrasting): direct application 36%; internal referral 24%; traditional networking only 7%; external referral 7% — [Kickresume](https://kickresume.com/en/press/nepotism-hiring-survey/)
- About 70% of professionals were hired at a company where they had a connection (attributed to a LinkedIn survey, secondary) — [Novoresume](https://novoresume.com/career-blog/networking-statistics); [Wavecnct](https://wavecnct.com/blogs/networking-statistics)
- Job-fair attendees (Minneapolis Fed 2023, n=224): about 75% were unemployed and about 20% were employed and looking. This shows career-event audiences skew unemployed, unlike conferences — [Minneapolis Fed](https://www.minneapolisfed.org/article/2024/job-fair-attendees-provide-new-insight-into-labor-market-challenges)

### Inferences
- The 60% figure appears to conflate "attend to network" (about 50%) with "attend to find a job." A defensible pitch claim would be: "about half of attendees list networking as a top reason, and a minority, unmeasured, are actively or passively job-seeking." The founder should validate this with their own attendee polls at 2-3 pilot events.
- Conference audiences are mostly employed, passive candidates. That makes them valuable to recruiters, but those people are discreet (see section 6).

### Gaps
- No Bizzabo, Freeman, CEIR or Eventbrite statistic on "attend to find a job" was found. No survey was found on the % of recruiters who attend industry conferences to source talent.

## 3. Recruiting economics (what employers pay today)

### Takeaway
Employers spend heavily per hire: SHRM 2025 puts it at about $5.5K average for non-executive roles and about $36K for executive roles. Agencies charge 15-25% (up to 30-35%) of first-year salary, and LinkedIn Recruiter seats run about $9-15K a year. A tool that produces even one qualified hire per event can justify a price of four to five figures per event for an employer. In-person career fairs are rated far more effective than virtual ones.

### Cited Findings
- SHRM 2025 Benchmarking (n=2,371, Jan-Mar 2025): average cost per hire **$5,475 non-executive and $35,879 executive**. Executive cost is up 21% since 2022 — [SHRM press release](https://www.shrm.org/in/about/press-room/shrm-releases-2025-benchmarking-reports--how-does-your-organizat). Note: the SHRM recruiting PDF reportedly shows different figures ($1,200 and $10,625), possibly a different metric (median?). Verify before citing — [SHRM PDF](https://www.shrm.org/content/dam/en/shrm/research/2025-recruiting-benchmarking-report.pdf)
- Agency contingency fees: typically 15-25% of first-year salary; 25-35% for tech, healthcare and executive roles; retained search 25-40%; flat fees $5K-$30K (vendor blogs, indicative) — [Manatal](https://www.manatal.com/blog/recruiter-commission); [Stealth Agents](https://stealthagents.com/research/cost-of-hiring-a-recruiter-2026)
- LinkedIn Recruiter (Corporate) is not publicly priced. Estimates run about $9K-$15K per seat per year ($10,800 and $12,960 cited). A UK Government Digital Marketplace listing shows $12,000 a year per license — [Pin](https://www.pin.com/blog/linkedin-recruiter-pricing-2026/); [TestGorilla](https://www.testgorilla.com/blog/linkedin-recruiter-pricing/); [Juicebox](https://juicebox.ai/blog/linkedin-recruiter-pricing)
- NACE: for 2023-24 intern recruiting, in-person fairs and on-campus recruiting were rated effective by more than two-thirds of employers, and virtual fairs by fewer than one-fifth — [NACE](https://naceweb.org/talent-acquisition/internships/in-person-activities-more-effective-than-virtual-ones-for-recruiting-interns)
- Employer participation in virtual fairs fell from 94.2% (2021) to 58.8% (2024). 93.9% of career centers will hold in-person fairs in 2024-25, and the median number of employers at in-person fairs rose to 109 — [NACE](https://naceweb.org/career-development/trends-and-predictions/employers-students-favor-in-person-career-fairs)

### Inferences
- Pricing anchor: if an event-based match yields a hire, value to the employer is roughly $5K (SHRM average) to $20K+ (agency fee on a $100K tech salary). The product could price as (a) a per-event recruiter pass of about $500-$2,000, comparable to lead retrieval plus booth extras, (b) a success fee well below agency rates (for example 5-8%), or (c) an annual employer subscription across events, positioned against a LinkedIn seat of about $10-12K.
- NACE data is for campus and early-career recruiting. Evidence that in-person beats virtual for experienced hires at professional conferences is not established.

### Gaps
- Career-fair booth costs and corporate recruiting-event budgets were not found from a reliable source. No hire-conversion rate for career fairs was found.

## 4. Event-tech market and how event apps make money

### Takeaway
Event management software is a roughly $7-16B market in 2025, depending on the analyst, growing about 9-15% a year. Event apps make money from **organizer licenses** (quote-based, often $3K-$9K+ a year at the low end), **exhibitor and sponsor upsells**, and especially **lead retrieval**, typically $350-$600 per exhibitor and $149-$200 per extra user, passed through by organizers.

### Cited Findings
- Market size 2025: MarketsandMarkets $16.4B, rising to $36.8B by 2031 (14.6% CAGR); IMARC $7.2B, rising to $14.7B by 2034; Roots Analysis $12.23B. Definitions differ widely — [MarketsandMarkets via Webull](https://www.webull.com/news/15243009160733696); [IMARC](https://www.imarcgroup.com/global-event-management-software-market); [Roots Analysis](https://www.rootsanalysis.com/event-management-software-market)
- Lead retrieval pricing (2024-2026 order forms): official app or device packages $350-$599 for the first unit and $149-$200 per extra user; API access $950-$990. A four-person booth team can exceed $1,000 per show — [Luminik](https://www.luminik.io/blog/2026/trade-show-lead-retrieval-costs-and-options.md); [PERIO 2026 form](https://am2026.perio.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/11/2026/03/LeadRetrievalPER261-Order-Form.pdf); [CHEST 2024](https://www.chestnet.org/-/media/annualmeeting/2024/documents/lead-retrieval-pricing-catalog.ashx)
- Organizers debate whether to charge exhibitors for lead retrieval or bundle it. It is a recognized organizer revenue line — [Converve](https://converve.com/event-networking-blog/lead-retrieval-for-event-organisers)
- Matchmaking apps (Brella, Grip) use quote-only pricing. Brella is reportedly geared to mid-to-large events and costly under 200 participants. Comparable apps: EventMobi from about $8,900 a year; Glue Up about $3,000 a year (third-party tracker) — [TrustRadius](https://www.trustradius.com/products/brella/pricing); [PulseSignal](https://getpulsesignal.com/pricing/brella)
- Grip reviewers note in-app sponsorship placements as a revenue lever — [Capterra](https://www.capterra.co.uk/software/173664/grip)

### Inferences
- Suggested model: **B2B2C**. Attendees use it for free. Employers and recruiters pay per event ("Talent Pass", priced like lead retrieval at about $500-$1,500), or pay for an annual multi-event subscription. Organizers get a revenue share (for example 20-30%) so they act as distribution channel, not gatekeeper. This copies how lead retrieval is sold through organizer order forms. Optional add-ons: sponsored "hiring lounge" or interview pods, and a featured-employer listing.
- A white-label module inside existing event apps (Brella, Grip, Cvent, Swapcard) may be a faster channel than a standalone app. Attendees already resist downloading another app. That resistance is an inference; no data was found.

### Gaps
- No public data was found on event-app adoption rates or on revenue splits between organizers and app vendors.

## 5. Trends 2024-2026: AI application spam, screening fatigue, return to in-person

### Takeaway
There is strong evidence for the founder's "human-first" thesis. Application volumes are up roughly 45%, driven by AI tools. Trust in AI screening is low among candidates. Candidate fraud and deepfakes are pushing employers back to **in-person interviews** (Gartner: 74% use in-person interviews to combat fraud).

### Cited Findings
- LinkedIn reportedly receives about 11,000 applications per minute, up 45% year over year (NYT, June 2025). Other reports cite about 9,500 a minute — [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/ai-job-applications-linkedin/); [Korn Ferry](https://www.kornferry.com/insights/this-week-in-leadership/the-application-avalanche); [Recruiting News Network](https://www.recruitingnewsnetwork.com/posts/recruiters-are-drinking-through-a-fire-hose-of-job-applications-experts-say--ai-is-partially-to-blame)
- Greenhouse 2025 AI in Hiring Report (Nov 2025, n=4,136 in US, UK, IE and DE): only 8% of job seekers say AI screening makes hiring fairer; 70% of hiring managers trust AI; 65% of US hiring managers have caught deceptive AI use; **39% of US hiring managers are doing more in-person interviews**; 74% of US job seekers use AI — [Greenhouse](https://www.greenhouse.com/newsroom/an-ai-trust-crisis-70-of-hiring-managers-trust-ai-to-make-faster-and-better-hiring-decisions-only-8-of-job-seekers-call-it-fair)
- Gartner (July 2025): only 26% of applicants trust AI to evaluate them fairly. In a 4Q24 survey, 39% of candidates used AI in applying. In a 1Q25 survey, only half trusted that the jobs they applied to were real — [Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-07-31-gartner-survey-shows-just-26-percent-of-job-applicants-trust-ai-will-fairly-evaluate-them)
- Gartner predicts that by 2028, **1 in 4 candidate profiles worldwide will be fake**. 6% of about 3,000 candidates (2Q25) admitted to interview fraud. Organizations are turning to video interviews (81%) and **in-person interviews (74%)** to combat fraud — [Gartner HR Symposium London Oct 2025](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-10-9-gartner-hr-symposium-xpo-london-day-3-highlights)
- Google, Cisco and McKinsey have reportedly reinstated in-person interview rounds because of AI-aided candidates (2025) — [HCAMag](https://www.hcamag.com/us/specialization/hr-technology/google-opts-for-in-person-interviews-amid-surge-in-ai-aided-candidates/545926)
- Gartner's TA trends for 2026 cite the AI revolution and cost pressures as the two driving forces — [Gartner](https://www.gartner.com/en/newsroom/press-releases/2025-10-07-gartner-says-ai-revolution-and-cost-pressures-are-two-forces-driving-the-top-four-trends-for-talent-acquisition-in-2026)

### Inferences
- An in-person meeting at a curated professional event comes with built-in identity verification (registered, badged attendee) and pre-filtering by industry. That is a strong differentiator in the anti-fraud and anti-AI-spam narrative.
- The cost-pressure trend cuts both ways: recruiting budgets are tight, so the product must prove a cost per qualified conversation below current channels.

### Gaps
- Skills-based hiring data (for example from TestGorilla or LinkedIn) was not retrieved. The "72% of recruiting leaders interview in person to fight fraud" figure circulates on blogs but was not confirmed in a primary source.

## 6. Risks: privacy and discretion, GDPR, organizer gatekeeping, sponsor poaching, legal

### Takeaway
The biggest product risk is **discretion**. Conference attendees are mostly employed, and their colleagues and employers are often at the same event. Even LinkedIn cannot guarantee hiding the "Open to Work" signal from a user's own employer. The other main risk is organizers and sponsors who may see poaching as a threat. GDPR and anti-discrimination risks are real but manageable through design.

### Cited Findings
- LinkedIn "Open to Work, recruiters only" tries to hide the signal from recruiters at the user's current company and affiliates but explicitly **cannot guarantee** it. Agency recruiters working for the employer may still see it — [Careerflow](https://www.careerflow.ai/blog/how-to-remove-open-to-work-on-linkedin); [The Interview Guys](https://blog.theinterviewguys.com/?p=11176)
- The public #OpenToWork badge is debated as possibly hurting candidates (stigma) — [Hiration](https://www.hiration.com/blog/does-open-to-work-hurt/)
- Exhibitors pay organizers for lead-capture access, so organizers control attendee data and app access. A third-party app needs organizer cooperation — [Converve](https://converve.com/event-networking-blog/lead-retrieval-for-event-organisers)

### Inferences
- Design requirements: (1) a private, recruiter-only visibility mode that automatically excludes the candidate's current employer and its known affiliates, plus a user-defined blocklist; (2) double opt-in matching, so identity is revealed only after mutual interest; (3) discreet meeting spots, such as off-floor interview pods, rather than a visible "jobs booth."
- GDPR (EU/UK) and Israeli privacy law: job-seeking status is personal data with high sensitivity in employment context. The app needs explicit consent, purpose limitation, data minimization and deletion after the event. Sharing data with an organizer requires controller and processor agreements. (Legal inference; no specific regulatory guidance was retrieved.)
- Anti-discrimination: quick, unstructured face-to-face screens raise bias risk (appearance, age, accent). Recommend structured question templates and logging. Employers remain liable under EEOC, EU and Israeli equal-employment law. (Inference; legal sources not retrieved.)
- Sponsors and exhibitors may object to competitors poaching their staff at the sponsor's event. Mitigations: give sponsors priority or exclusive "hiring partner" status, let organizers choose whether to enable the feature, and position it as a sponsor benefit.

### Gaps
- No survey was found quantifying employees' fear of employers discovering their job search. No case law or regulator guidance specific to event-based recruiting apps was found. No data on sponsor attitudes to talent poaching at events was found.
