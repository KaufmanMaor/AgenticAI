# Competitive landscape: on-site recruiter-to-job-seeker matching at professional conferences

Research date: 2026-10-13. About 17 web searches; few full-page fetches, so many findings rest on search snippets of vendor or third-party pages (flagged where relevant). Training-data knowledge is labelled "unverified" and kept to Inferences or Gaps.

## 1. Event networking and matchmaking apps: intent tags such as "hiring" or "looking for a job", AI matchmaking, meeting scheduling, pricing

### Takeaway
Brella has a built-in "recruiting" intent that matches job seekers with recruiters and turns matches into scheduled 1:1 meetings with an assigned location, so the core mechanic already exists as a feature. b2match and Swapcard are sold for job fairs too. All of them are general B2B event-networking tools that the organizer licenses per event or per year. None is a standalone hiring product aimed at attendees.

### Cited Findings
- **Brella**: matchmaking is built on intents (networking, trading, investing, mentoring and recruiting). The recruiting intent "connects people looking for a job with a recruiter AND vice versa" and can be used at any event, whatever the industry. Brella advises organizers that it helps sponsors find key people when recruiters attend — [Brella help: use cases beyond networking](https://help.brella.io/en/organizers/matchmaking-use-cases-beyond-networking); [Brella organizer help](https://help-organizers.brella.io/en/articles/177660-use-cases-beyond-networking)
- Brella: for associations, its matchmaking "filters attendees and connects job seekers to prospective employers" — [Brella associations](https://www.brella.io/associations)
- Brella meeting flow: the attendee picks a time and sends a request, and the location is assigned automatically. Meetings need mutual acceptance before they enter the schedule. Brella's product focus is now in-person and hybrid events — [IDGA how to use Brella](https://www.idga.org/events-counteruas-usa/our-online-platform); [Brella company info](https://www.brella.io/company-information)
- **Grip**: priced on request. A third-party directory puts the entry point at about £2,500 per event (app, matchmaking engine, meeting management, analytics), with annual licenses for organizers who run several events. It claims "16 parallel algorithms" over intent, industry, product interest and behaviour (vendor-adjacent claim) — [KI-Syndikat Grip](https://www.ki-syndikat.de/tools/grip/); [G2 Grip pricing](https://www.g2.com/products/grip-events/pricing)
- **Swapcard**: listed from about $570–$610 per user per year on Capterra and GetApp. G2 lists an entry tier "starting at $610, 200 attendees". Swapcard markets AI matchmaking and lead capture — [Capterra Swapcard](https://www.capterra.com/p/234990/Swapcard/); [GetApp Swapcard](https://www.getapp.com/sales-software/a/swapcard/); [G2 Swapcard vs Whova](https://www.g2.com/compare/swapcard-vs-whova)
- Swapcard was used for IÉSEG Career Day 2020 (60+ companies, 600 students). Recruiters viewed candidate profiles, booked 1:1 appointments and hosted virtual stands — [IÉSEG](https://www.ieseg.fr/en/?p=101409)
- **Whova**: no published pricing. It charges a custom per-event fee plus a ticketing fee of 3.0% + $0.99 per paid ticket (third-party review). It offers AI recommendations for networking — [Lineupr Whova pricing review](https://lineupr.com/en/blog/whova-event-app-pricing-review); [Toolradar Whova](https://toolradar.com/tools/whova/pricing)
- **b2match**: sells explicit "career fair / job fair" software for in-person, hybrid and virtual events. Participant-type selection at signup (e.g. company vs candidate), custom registration questions that attendees can filter on, searchable job boards and 1:1 meeting scheduling — [b2match job fair use case](https://www.b2match.com/use-cases/job-fair); [b2match career fair blog](https://www.b2match.com/de/blog/career-fair-solution)
- **Hopin / RingCentral Events**: RingCentral bought Hopin Events and Hopin Sessions in August 2023 for a reported $15M, possibly up to $50M with earn-outs per SEC filings. A ro.am page claims about $200M, which conflicts with these. The product was rebranded RingCentral Events — [Skift Meetings](https://meetings.skift.com/2023/08/09/hopin-events-and-session-products-sold-for-15-million/); [UC Today](https://www.uctoday.com/collaboration/ringcentral-acquires-virtual-events-assets-from-hopin/); [Medium on SEC filings](https://medium.com/@OnBridger/hopin-sold-for-15-million-after-raising-1-billion-9b9d1a8ae4b6)
- **Braindate (e180, Montréal)**: a topic-based peer-learning meeting platform for conferences. G2 shows reviews as late as Dec 2025, so it appears active — [mtl.org Braindate](https://meet.mtl.org/en/blog/braindate); [G2 e180](https://www.g2.com/pt/sellers/e180)
- **Lunchclub**: AI-matched 1:1 professional video meetings. It was valued at over $100M in 2020. The App Store listing still exists, and I found no shutdown report — [CNBC 2020](https://www.cnbc.com/2020/09/01/lunchclub-tops-100-million-valuation-amid-quarantine-usage-spike.html); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id1538817081)

### Inferences
- The "hiring vs job-seeking intent, then match, then meet" mechanic already exists inside Brella, and partly inside b2match and Swapcard, as one feature of an organizer-licensed event app. So the matching logic itself is not new. The differentiation would have to come from elsewhere: attendee-initiated use at any event without the organizer buying in, real-time "who is here now", the interview format, and remote recruiters.
- Pricing across this category is B2B to the organizer (per event, from about £2.5k for Grip; per-user annual for Swapcard; per-event plus ticket fee for Whova). Job seekers and recruiters do not pay directly. A product sold to recruiters (pay per hire, per booth or per event pass) would sit in a different pricing lane.
- The intent tag only works if the organizer turns it on and recruiters actually attend and opt in. Most conference organizers do not market their event as a hiring venue.

### Gaps
- I did not check Jublia, Bizzabo, Cvent, Eventbrite, Luma, Zerista, Hubilo or Airmeet for a "hiring" intent tag. Unverified recollection: Cvent and Bizzabo offer attendee-to-attendee appointment scheduling, Luma is a lightweight event and RSVP tool with no matchmaking, and Jublia (Singapore) does AI business matching. None of these confirmed hiring-specific tags.
- No independent data on how much the Brella recruiting intent is used, or how well it works.
- Grip has no recruitment case study, and I found no current per-attendee pricing for Grip or Brella.

## 2. Job matching at conferences themselves (Web Summit, Collision, CES, SXSW, GHC, re:Invent, KubeCon, DEF CON, Cybertech, Israeli conferences)

### Takeaway
Most flagship conferences have no live, in-venue job matching. The exceptions are Grace Hopper Celebration (resume database, interview booths and on-the-spot offers in the Expo, the closest real-world precedent) and KubeCon/CNCF (a free online job board, not tied to the venue). Web Summit's app matches startups and investors, not candidates and employers. Israeli tech hiring happens at university career fairs, not at conferences such as Cybertech.

### Cited Findings
- **Grace Hopper Celebration (AnitaB.org)**: a resume database lets sponsor companies reach candidates before and after the event. Sponsors meet candidates at "Interview Booths" in the Expo and can make offers on the spot. Recruiters schedule interviews in an on-site Interview Hall, and some attendees got every interview by handing out resumes at the Expo and being booked on the spot — [ghc.anitab.org](https://ghc.anitab.org/fr/?p=118111); [W&M MSBA 2018](https://mason.wm.edu/news/2018/grace-hopper-from-the-msba-perspective.php); [Two Sigma](https://www.twosigma.com/articles/how-to-get-an-interview-at-the-grace-hopper-celebration)
- **KubeCon / CNCF**: the CNCF job board launched at KubeCon NA in November 2019. It is free for employers and applicants (members get featured listings). Seekers post profiles, and employers search by location or skill. A KubeCon EU page described an AWS-sponsored virtual job board open to all attending companies — [CNCF announcement](https://www.cncf.io/announcements/2019/11/19/cloud-native-computing-foundation-announces-cncf-job-board/); [Linux Foundation events](https://events.linuxfoundation.org/hyperledger-global-forum/attend/experiences/)
- **Web Summit**: the official app is built for networking. Attendees register attributes such as "start-up" or "investor" and search for contacts. I found no hiring feature. A third-party startup (Digital Factory) launched a "Hire Me" video-pitch recruiting app at Web Summit around 2019, and its current status is unknown — [Forbes Portugal](https://www.forbespt.com/software-da-web-summit-vai-potenciar-a-aproximacao-de-empresas-e-investidores); [Ver Angola](https://www.verangola.net/va/pt/112019/Tecnologia/17157/‘Hire-Me’-chega-a-Angola-para-acabar-com-os-CV’s-e-tornar-o-recrutamento-mais-simples.htm)
- **Israel**: the Tel Aviv University Technology Career Fair (7 January 2026) is billed as "the largest career fair in Israel". Dozens of high-tech companies recruit there and résumés are submitted on the spot. I found nothing on Cybertech Tel Aviv recruiting features — [TAU alumni](https://en-alumni.tau.ac.il/Technology_Career_Fair_Jan26)

### Inferences
- GHC proves the demand: recruiters will travel to a conference and interview there. But GHC runs it as a heavy, sponsor-paid process (resume database, booked interview hall) for one community, which leaves room for a lightweight tool that works at any event.
- Conference organizers seem to treat jobs as a side feature (a static board) and not as a matching layer, maybe because hiring is not their core revenue line and it can upset sponsors who don't want their staff poached.

### Gaps
- No sources found for CES, SXSW (unverified recollection: SXSW has run job and career sessions, but I found nothing about a job fair), AWS re:Invent, DEF CON (it has informal recruiting and a "Career Hacking Village" that I could not verify), Collision (renamed Web Summit Vancouver in 2024, unverified), Cybertech, or other Israeli conferences such as AI Week or Calcalist events.
- Which platform GHC uses in 2026 is not confirmed.

## 3. Career fair and recruiting-event platforms (Handshake, Brazen/Radancy, vFairs, Easy Virtual Fair, CareerEco, Symplicity, Paradox, Indeed, Fairs app, etc.)

### Takeaway
A mature category serves dedicated career fairs. It covers QR booth check-in (Handshake), conversational AI registration and on-site interview scheduling (Paradox), and virtual fair platforms with booth video chats (vFairs, Brazen, CareerEco, Easy Virtual Fair). All of them assume the event is a hiring event that a school, employer or workforce board organizes. None of them overlays hiring onto an ordinary professional conference.

### Cited Findings
- **Handshake**: generates a QR check-in sign for each employer booth. Students scan it with or without the app, employers get a summary email of check-ins after the fair, and they can download profiles and resumes — [Handshake blog](https://joinhandshake.com/blog/career-centers/career-fairs-and-employer-booth-check-ins); [Handshake support](https://support.joinhandshake.com/hc/en-us/articles/360045426034-Check-in-with-an-Employer-at-an-Event-or-Fair); [Cal Poly Pomona](https://careercenter.cpp.edu/blog/2024/02/11/how-to-utilize-the-employer-booth-qr-codes-at-career-fairs/)
- **Paradox (Conversational Events)**: registration through a QR code, landing page, SMS keyword or short code, or an on-site kiosk mode, run by the AI assistant "Olivia". It automates reminders, screening, Q&A and instant scheduling of 1:1, panel, virtual or in-person interviews, and supports job fairs, campus events and virtual events. Pricing is custom and sales-led — [Paradox events](https://www.paradox.ai/products/events); [SoftwareOne listing](https://platform.softwareone.com/product/conversational-events/PCP-2989-0846); [Makerstack review](https://makerstack.co/reviews/paradox-review/)
- **vFairs**: per-event licensing with optional annual contracts, "typically $5,000–$25,000+ per event". This range comes from a competitor (Easy Virtual Fair) comparison page — [EasyVirtualFair vs vFairs](https://easyvirtualfair.com/compare/easyvirtualfair-vs-vfairs/); [ZoftwareHub vFairs pricing](https://zoftwarehub.com/products/vfairs/pricing)
- **CareerEco**: a virtual recruiting platform founded in 2009, based in Atlanta and known for grad-school fairs. It hosts career fairs and hiring events and announced a feature to support onsite career fairs — [CareerEco](https://events.careereco.com/careereco-releases-new-technology-feature-to-support-onsite-career-fairs/); [G2 CareerEco](https://www.g2.com/products/careereco/discuss)
- **The Fairs App**: spun out of the Colorado State University Career Center (built in 2016). It offers employer profiles, filters by major and role, favourites and a fair map, is free for students, and "tens of thousands" of attendees have used it. It is preparation and navigation only, with no matching — [CSU Source](https://source.colostate.edu/the-fairs-app-improving-the-career-fair-experience-for-everyone); [Bentley](https://careeredge.bentley.edu/blog/2020/01/31/69200/)
- **Speed-interview formats**: Bell runs about 10-minute 1:1 speed interviews in virtual booth chat rooms, where candidates book slots and recruiters start the call. PrepLounge runs remote "speed recruiting" weeks with consultancies over video. NowMax (Belgium, founded 2018, 1–10 staff) makes a remote video-interview app — [Bell speed interviews](https://technicalskills-careerevent.bell.ca/en/speed-interviews); [PrepLounge](https://www.preplounge.com/en/speed-recruiting/Consulting_Apr_24); [NowMax](https://jobs.imecistart.com/companies/nowmax)
- Symplicity Recruit and CareerEco both have G2 pricing pages. Neither showed a public price in the snippets — [G2 Symplicity](https://www.g2.com/products/symplicity-recruit/pricing); [G2 CareerEco](https://www.g2.com/products/careereco/pricing)

### Inferences
- The pieces of the proposed idea all exist separately: QR check-in (Handshake), AI-scheduled on-site interviews (Paradox), booth video speed interviews with remote recruiters (Bell, vFairs-style virtual booths). Nobody has combined them for attendees of a general conference.
- Paradox is aimed at high-volume hourly hiring (e.g. a Wendy's franchise case study on paradox.ai). Handshake is aimed at campus hiring. Neither targets experienced professionals at industry conferences.

### Gaps
- Brazen (Radancy), Yello, Gem events, Jobvite/Employ events and Easy Virtual Fair pricing were not found. Indeed Hiring Events is unverified: my recollection is that Indeed scaled the product back around 2023, but I could not confirm it. Fairs.ai and JobsOnTheSpot returned nothing.
- No traction metrics found for Handshake fairs, Paradox Events or vFairs beyond vendor claims.

## 4. Startups doing location-based hiring at events (proximity, BLE, QR check-in)

### Takeaway
I found no startup whose product is "see hiring and job-seeking attendees physically at this venue right now, then meet and interview". The closest analogues are Brella's recruiting intent (organizer-dependent, scheduled meetings rather than live presence), Handshake booth QR check-in, Paradox QR or kiosk registration, and older Web Summit add-ons such as "Hire Me".

### Cited Findings
- A targeted search for proximity- or event-based recruiter and job-seeker apps found only general job apps. **Switch** offered anonymous double-opt-in, swipe-style job matching, not location- or event-based (Wikipedia notes it is older and has issues). **Wellfound** and **Underdog.io** are city-level marketplaces — [Switch (app) Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switch_(app)); [Wellfound](https://wellfound.com/jobs); [Underdog.io](https://landing.underdog.io/)
- "Hire Me" (Digital Factory) launched at Web Summit as a video-pitch recruiting app with hashtag skills. I found no evidence of later traction — [Ver Angola](https://www.verangola.net/va/pt/112019/Tecnologia/17157/‘Hire-Me’-chega-a-Angola-para-acabar-com-os-CV’s-e-tornar-o-recrutamento-mais-simples.htm)

### Inferences
- The lack of a surviving, visible player is itself a signal. It could be an open gap, or it could mean earlier attempts failed on liquidity: too few recruiters at any one event, and conference organizers controlling attendee data and the app. Founders should look into that history.
- Unverified recollection, not sourced here: proximity networking apps such as Highlight, Sonar and Bizzabo's early "nearby" features (around 2012–2014) and BLE beacon networking at conferences existed and largely faded, which hints at problems with retention or density.

### Gaps
- No outcome data (shutdowns, funding) on any event-specific hiring startup could be confirmed. Searching Crunchbase or ProductHunt for "career fair app" and "conference hiring app" is recommended.

## 5. Gaps: what none of them do

### Takeaway
No existing product combines (a) a 2-minute, attendee-initiated profile that works at any professional conference without the organizer buying the platform, (b) a binary hiring vs job-seeking intent with a live "who's here now" view, (c) an instant on-the-spot interview, and (d) a speed-interview booth that brings in remote recruiters by live video.

### Cited Findings
- Event networking apps that support recruiting intents need the organizer to license and configure them (Brella intents; b2match participant types; Grip and Swapcard sold per event or per user to organizers) — [Brella](https://help.brella.io/en/organizers/matchmaking-use-cases-beyond-networking); [b2match](https://www.b2match.com/use-cases/job-fair); [KI-Syndikat Grip](https://www.ki-syndikat.de/tools/grip/); [Capterra Swapcard](https://www.capterra.com/p/234990/Swapcard/)
- Brella's meeting flow is request, time slot, mutual accept and assigned location, which means pre-scheduled slots rather than ad-hoc "meet now" — [IDGA](https://www.idga.org/events-counteruas-usa/our-online-platform)
- Remote video speed interviews exist only in virtual fairs (Bell, PrepLounge, vFairs-type platforms). None is a physical booth at an in-person conference connecting to off-site recruiters — [Bell](https://technicalskills-careerevent.bell.ca/en/speed-interviews); [PrepLounge](https://www.preplounge.com/en/speed-recruiting/Consulting_Apr_24)
- Conference job features that do exist are static boards (CNCF) or heavy sponsor programs (GHC interview hall) — [CNCF](https://www.cncf.io/announcements/2019/11/19/cloud-native-computing-foundation-announces-cncf-job-board/); [GHC](https://ghc.anitab.org/fr/?p=118111)

### Inferences
- **Unserved white space:**
  1. An organizer-independent layer that attendees bring themselves (QR or link at the venue, geofenced to the event), so it works at Cybertech, AWS Summit Tel Aviv and similar events without a platform contract.
  2. Real-time presence: who with the opposite intent is in the building now.
  3. Hybrid remote-recruiter booths, so companies that are not exhibiting can still recruit at the event.
  4. Pricing aimed at recruiters (per event pass or per interview) instead of organizers.
- **Main risks that competitors' structure suggests:**
  1. Organizers may resist a parallel app, or want a revenue share, because they sell sponsor packages that include recruiting access (GHC model).
  2. Brella, Swapcard or Grip could ship a "hiring now" toggle quickly, since intent matching is already built.
  3. There may be too few recruiters at any one event.
  4. Candidates who are employed may worry about privacy if they show up as "job seeking" at an industry event where their employer is also present. The anonymous double opt-in model (Switch) is relevant here.
- **Israel note:** in the sources found, Israeli tech hiring is concentrated at university fairs (TAU) and online tools such as Jobify. There is no conference-embedded hiring product. Dense, frequent tech conferences in Tel Aviv could make a pilot market, though this is unvalidated.

### Gaps
- I did not research employer willingness to pay, or the legal and privacy constraints of on-the-spot interviews (e.g. Israeli privacy law, GDPR at EU events).
