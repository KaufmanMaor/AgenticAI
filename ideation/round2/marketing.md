# Round 2: Marketing stress-test of Concept v2

*VP Marketing. v2 is much better than the original pitch. Below is what still breaks, from the go-to-market side.*

## 1. The three biggest flaws

**1. The whole funnel depends on one email from a brand nobody knows.** Pre-event matching ("N relevant people are coming") is what makes recruiters buy. But it needs employed candidates to opt in 72 hours early, from an organizer email, to a brand with no history and no proof. That is the weakest step in the funnel, and v2 has no plan for it. If the opt-in rate is 3%, recruiters see "4 people" and the ₪3,500 sale dies. **The pre-event candidate campaign is the product, not an add-on.**

**2. The privacy promises contradict each other.** v2 promises three things that don't fit together:
- data dies after 72 hours
- the north star is measured over 14 days
- one-tap ATS export plus a "Hired in the Hallway" story engine

On top of that, profiles open *before* the event, while the pitch says you're in "only because you're physically here". One screenshot of a broken promise in a dev WhatsApp group and we're done. The promise has to be one line we can audit: **"Nothing about you leaves the event unless you say yes to a specific company."**

**3. Sponsor-first selling looks like "the organizer sold me to its sponsors".** Sponsors get "24-hour early access" and sponsors are the first buyers. To a candidate, that reads as attendee data being sold. The mechanic is fine; how it's described is toxic. Sell sponsors **first pick of coffee slots and a branded corner**, never "access to candidates". Related point: the required salary band is our best candidate hook ("see who pays your range"), but some Israeli companies will refuse to publish one. Show bands only inside the event app, never on a public page, and treat that as a sales objection we have to beat, not drop.

## 2. Tension #5: Hooked, and earning passive-candidate trust

**Recommendation: one learning coffee with Noa Barazani. No partnership, license or co-brand in year 1.**
- **Against a partnership:**
  - The dating association is exactly the trust problem we're solving: HR legal teams would flag it, and employed engineers would picture being "on display".
  - Hooked's assets (QR entry, a "who's here" list) are the cheap part; Tech scoped them at 8–10 weeks.
  - What's expensive is recruiter sales and the Employer Shield, and Hooked has neither.
  - Its roughly 15K users are singles at parties, not our audience.
- **What's worth getting from them:** what share of attendees actually scan the QR, and which activation tactics work. That is the number our unit economics depend on (BizDev assumes 35%). An exit or acquisition story with them can come later.
- **Housekeeping:** stop saying "Hooked for jobs" in any external material, today. It invites a passing-off complaint and puts the wrong picture in people's heads.

**How we earn trust when we have no history:**
1. **Borrow trust from the community.** Co-brand, don't white-label: "Hosted by DevOpsDays TLV · powered by Hallway". The community gives credibility, and we keep the brand across events.
2. **Show it, don't claim it.** The landing page shows a live preview of "what a company sees before you knock: nothing", plus "Your employer: **[Company] blocked**" right after you enter your employer.
3. **Real people.** The founders' faces and a phone number. Three respected Israeli platform/DevOps leads publicly vouch for it ("I checked how it works").
4. **Put deletion in writing.** A Hebrew one-page "privacy in 5 lines" reviewed by a lawyer, and a WhatsApp deletion receipt after the event.
5. **A no-ghosting pledge from companies.** They reply within 48 hours or the candidate is told and the company is flagged.
6. **Seed with groups that have little to hide:** returning reservists, olim, people moving back from abroad. They bring density and early stories without the risk.

## 3. Naming: final recommendation

**Spoken brand "Hallway". Registered mark "Hallway Talent". Hebrew sub-brand "מסדרון" (Misderon) for Israeli events.**

Conflict checks (web search only; the network blocked registrar lookups, so domains still need checking by hand):
- **Hallway:** a generic word with several small users: an avatar/virtual-meetings startup backed by Sequoia Arc (Columbia, MO, about 5 people), Hallway Studios, and "The Hallway", a Sydney agency (which ran a QR recruiting stunt). There is no recruiting/HR product called Hallway. The only adjacent one is a 2023 student pitch. That means a weak standalone trademark but low confusion in HR services, so register the composite.
- **Misderon / מסדרון:** no app or brand found.
- **Domains to try, in order:** hallway.jobs, hallwaytalent.com, hallway.co.il, misderon.co.il. Buy them before any PR.
- **Feature names:** Knock, Coffee Slot, Employer Shield, Hallway Pass. "Knock" works as a feature but not as a brand (the word is crowded).

## 5. Where I still disagree

- **The beachhead is five events across three niches.** It should be **one niche (DevOps/platform/SRE) for 3 events.** The same companies come back, the same candidates trust us, and every story compounds. The Cyber Week lounge belongs in year 2; it skews toward government and abroad.
- **The "Hired in the Hallway" story engine isn't a v1 feature.** It's marketing work done by hand, with consent, 90 days after the hire, and told by the employer.
- **The "Speed InterviewDate" booth stunt at event 3** is fine as PR, but only after we have hire stories. A stunt with nothing behind it gets mocked.
- **Mid-level 3–8 years:** I accept HR's targeting, but the brand voice stays "peer-to-peer, senior". Mid-level people want to feel courted, not processed.
- **72-hour deletion and the 14-day north star:** pick one public promise (see flaw 2), and let the metric come from opted-in surveys only.
