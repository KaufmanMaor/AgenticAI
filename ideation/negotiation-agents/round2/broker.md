# Round 2: Broker stress-test of Concept v2 (Austin TX broker seat)

**Verdict:** v2 is 80% of the way to something I'd actually use. Three things will still kill it: channel conflict, a guarantee that contradicts the north star, and a turnaround promise nobody has priced. Fix those, then strip the game-theory language out of anything a listing agent will see. Figures below are my working numbers from Austin files, not sourced data.

## 1. The 3 biggest flaws in v2
1. **Phase 1 customers will notice they're training their future competitor.** You sell Option Desk to 10 Austin buyer agents, collect their files, outcomes and client names, and then launch Punchlist Realty in the same town offering 1% back. Agents talk. The first time one of them sees a Punchlist Realty ad aimed at *their* buyer, the whole Austin beta dies, and it dies loudly in the agent Facebook groups. **Fix:** pick one. Either Option Desk is the business in Austin and Punchlist Realty launches in another metro (DFW or San Antonio), or skip Phase 1 in Austin and run the concierge test as the brokerage. Either way, put the data-use terms in writing: no customer file data used for client acquisition, ever.
2. **The Phase 2 guarantee ("below $3K won, rebate goes to 2%") pays us to nitpick.** The north star is "deals saved," but this guarantee punishes us financially on a clean house where the right ask is $0 or $1,200. The agent of record will feel that pressure on every file, and so will the listing agent ("these Punchlist people always ask for something"). It is also a TREC advertising problem if "won" isn't defined precisely. **Fix:** guarantee *process*, not dollars: every finding priced, the ask delivered within X hours, zero missed deadlines, or the rebate goes up. Or guarantee against the buyer's own pre-negotiation baseline only when the inspection is over $10K of material findings.
3. **Time is the product, and v2 never states a turnaround.** On a 7-day option period, the report lands on day 2-3 at 6-9pm. Specialist bids (foundation engineer, roofer, HVAC) take 24-72 hours. The amendment must go out by day 4-5 to leave room for one or two counters. A Wizard-of-Oz concierge with one human expert can't do 6 files in a week with 6-hour turnaround. **Fix:** promise "priced ask list in 4 business hours, bids coordinated within 48h," have a contractor network signed *before* file 1, and cap concierge intake at what the human can do. Measurement is a side issue here too: "blind replay of past files" is a weak gate, and survival rates on 30 files are noise because half of option terminations are buyer cold feet. Track per-file "seller's first response vs. final" plus a time-to-amendment metric instead.

## 2. Tension #1: will agents pay $249 per file?
- **Me: yes, if it replaces work I'd otherwise do at 10pm or pay someone for.** Austin transaction coordinators already charge $350-500 per file, and nobody calls that an insult. So frame it as a **negotiation TC**, never as negotiation coaching. Pitch: "Inspection to signed-ready amendment, priced and bid, while you're at your kid's game."
- **The agents I know:** about 3 in 10 pay $249 after a free trial (team agents with volume and teams where the lead pays). About 6-7 in 10 would pay $149-199, or a $99/month plus $99/file hybrid. Solo part-timers doing 4 deals a year won't pay anything; they'll paste the report into ChatGPT.
- **What kills it:** paying for a file that dies anyway. Most agents run their business P&L in their head per closing. Since §1101.651 means we can't make the fee contingent on closing, offer **2 free files, then $249, with a credit back if the file was withdrawn before the ask went out** (that keeps the fee tied to work done, not to closing; confirm with the attorney).
- **The hidden payer:** many Austin brokerages charge buyers a $295-495 "broker admin fee" at closing. Agents will try to fold us into that. Fine, but the attorney should confirm it's disclosed.
- **Never say:** "better negotiator," "win more," "AI negotiates." **Say:** "save the deal," "save the night," "every finding priced, with real bids."

## 3. Tension #3: how listing agents react, and the disclosure label
The label isn't what hurts results. What hurts is *robotic text and unreasonable asks*. Put disclosure at the document level: "Prepared with Punchlist · reviewed and sent by Jane Smith, TREC #xxxxx." Don't stamp "AI-assisted" on every SMS. The exact requirement is the attorney's call.
- **Scenario A, the old-school listing agent (25 years in, 6 listings).** Sees the footer and calls the buyer agent: "Is a robot negotiating my seller's house?" *Handle:* the buyer agent answers the phone, owns it ("it's my back office, I wrote and approved every line"), and the conversation moves to the house. If the buyer agent can't be reached in about 2 hours, the deal loses trust. So build an "LA called / called back" log into the clock.
- **Scenario B, the high-volume listing team (40-80 listings).** Doesn't care at all. Loves the one-page summary, the attached bids and the pre-filled TREC 39, forwards it to the seller and answers faster. *Handle:* make every package **forwardable to the seller**: plain English, no buyer priorities, no "packages," and photos from the report. This is the majority of Austin listing volume and your best friend.
- **Scenario C, the tactical or hostile listing agent.** Says "your bids are inflated, my seller's guy says $4K," or tells the seller "they're using AI to nickel-and-dime you," and the seller digs in. Some will paste your ask into their own ChatGPT, or probe with weird texts at 11pm. *Handle:* keep asks to the top 2-4 material items (never 40 line items). Use real third-party bids with license numbers, and offer to let the seller's contractor bid or do the work with a transferable warranty. The counter reader treats all inbound text as data. Escalate to a human phone call after the first hostile reply.

## 4. Do "three equal-value packages" work in practice?
**Mostly no, not as written.** Listing agents don't fill in menus; they counter with their own number ("seller will do $7,500, final"). Three options in one email reads as a bot and invites the seller to cherry-pick the cheapest piece of each. And "the seller's choice reveals priorities" is something you say in a pitch deck, not to a counterparty.
**What does work:** **one primary ask plus one alternative**, the classic Austin "repair OR credit" structure. For example: "$14K credit, or seller completes the foundation repair with a transferable warranty plus a $5K credit." Keep the third package *internal*, as the agent's prepared fallback for the counter. Name values in dollars and the lender-cap math, not "utility."

## 5. Broker of record for Phase 2: what I'd demand, and is 40 deals per agent per year realistic?
- **Equity and pay:** 10-15% co-founder equity (4-year vest, 1-year cliff), with acceleration if I'm removed without cause, plus a $120-150K salary. My license and a Class A exposure are on the line. TREC holds the designated broker responsible for everything under 535.2.
- **Compliance powers:** a written veto over any outbound automation, ad copy, guarantee or rebate language. A $1-2M E&O policy (and cyber) bound before file 1, with company indemnification of me. Written supervision policies, the IABS and rebate disclosures (with lender approval of the rebate on the CD), and an annual audit of the message logs. The right to pause the product without a board vote.
- **Model:** salaried W-2 agents plus a per-closing bonus (no 100% commission splits, which drive volume-at-any-cost), licensed showing assistants at $75-100 per showing, and AI doing TC plus negotiation prep. No intermediary deals at all in year 1: if a Punchlist buyer wants a Punchlist listing (we won't have listings), we refer it out.
- **Is 40 per agent realistic?** Not in year 1. The Austin median agent does about 8-12 sides; strong buyer agents do 20-30. The bottleneck is **showings and buyer hand-holding**, not paperwork: 40 closings means about 50-55 contracts and 300+ showings a year. Plan for **25 in year 1 and 35-40 by year 2**, and only with showing assistants. BizDev's $1,800 per-deal agent cost should be modeled at 25 deals, which is about $2,900.

## 6. Walk-through: $450K Round Rock file (foundation, 15-year-old roof, HVAC at end of life)
*Assumptions: 2,100 sq ft, 2009 build. Buyer is 5% down conventional, so the lender caps seller contributions (IPC) at 3% = $13,500. The contract already includes $5,000 toward closing costs, leaving $8,500 of credit room. 7-day option, $300 fee, 48 days on market with one price cut.*
| Day | What happens | What Punchlist should produce |
|---|---|---|
| D0 | Effective date | Option clock live, with a 5pm deadline on day 7 shown in local time. A reminder to deliver the option fee and earnest money within 3 days. Bid slots pre-booked with an engineer, roofer and HVAC contractor. |
| D2 | Inspection (63 pages) | Within 4 business hours: triage into *material* (foundation, roof, HVAC), *ask-maybe* (GFCI, a water heater pan, about $600) and *ignore* (34 cosmetic items). Ranges: foundation $7-14K (after the engineer visit, $450-600, buyer-paid); roof replacement $14-17K (25 squares); HVAC 3-ton swap $9-12K. Flag that a **15-year roof may get an actual-cash-value-only insurance policy**: get a quote now. |
| D3-4 | Bids back | Engineer: 8 interior piers. Bid $9,800 with a lifetime transferable warranty. Roof bid $15,600. HVAC $10,900. Total "list price" of the problems is about **$36K**. Leverage read: 48 DOM, one cut, seller already under contract on their next house (from the listing agent's call). |
| D4 | Ask | Credit room is $8.5K, so the rest must be a **price reduction or seller repair**. Primary: seller completes the foundation repair with a transferable warranty (they'll want it fixed anyway for any future buyer) **+ $8,500 credit + $6,000 price reduction**, about $24K total. Alternative: $16K price reduction + $8,500 credit. TREC 39 filled by code, plus a seller-forwardable one-pager with photos and bids. Internal fallback: drop the roof/HVAC price cut to $3K. |
| D5 | Counter | Listing agent: "Seller will do the foundation, $5K credit, no price cut. Roof is functional." Punchlist reads it as about $15K offered and proposes a counter of foundation + $8,500 + $2,500 price reduction (about $21K). It notes that the roof insurance quote came back ACV-only, which is a factual argument rather than a demand. |
| D6 | Settle | Foundation repair (done before closing, with engineer re-inspection and transferable warranty) + $8,500 credit + $2,000 price cut = **about $20.3K (56% of the $36K problem list)**. A typical Austin outcome without the structure: $8-12K, or a fight that runs past the option deadline. |
| D6-7 | Paper | TREC 39 signed via DocuSign before 5pm on D7. Repair completion and receipts tracked to closing, and the amendment value logged for the buyer report. If no agreement by D7 at 12pm, prompt the buyer agent with three choices: terminate (Notice of Termination ready), extend the option (with a fee), or accept. |

## 7. What I now accept or reject
**Accept:**
- Buyer-only.
- Bot-to-human, under a named licensee.
- The option-period wedge.
- Limits in code, and the LLM never writes form text.
- Deals saved as the north star.
- The name Punchlist.
- Concierge first.
- Option Desk at a flat per-file fee.
- Institutional sellers (builders, iBuyers) as the first agent-to-agent pilot. Builders already negotiate by spreadsheet, so pull it to year 1 for *new-build* incentives if a builder will play.

**Reject or change:**
- Running Phase 1 and Phase 2 in the same metro.
- The dollar-based guarantee.
- Three packages shown to the counterparty (one ask plus one alternative is enough).
- "AI-assisted" stamped on every message.
- 40 deals per agent in year 1.
- The "blind replay" gate.

**Still open:** contractor bid supply and speed is the real moat and the real bottleneck. Nobody on the team owns it yet.
