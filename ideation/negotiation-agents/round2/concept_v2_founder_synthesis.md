# Concept v2: Founder synthesis after Round 1

*Written by the founder after reading the Round 1 inputs from PM, Marketing, BizDev, Tech, the Texas broker and the RE attorney. This is the version the team now stress-tests. US only, Austin first.*

## What I'm keeping from Omri's idea
- **People and their realtors lose money and deals in negotiation.** The data backs this. About 15% of US contracts cancel, and 17–22% in some Texas metros. Inspection and repair disputes are the leading cause, and the broker confirms that the post-inspection amendment is where deals blow up.
- **An AI agent that is a better negotiator than the human,** one that hunts for win-win trades (repairs vs. credits, closing date, leaseback) instead of a pure price fight.
- **The agent reports back and the principal decides** ("the seller will fix the roof if you drop the fence ask").
- **Long-term vision:** agents talking to agents.

## What I'm changing (the team convinced me)
1. **One side, not two.** We represent the buyer only. Two agents from one company negotiating with each other is a Texas intermediary deal. It needs written consent, appointed licensees and clean rooms, the stronger model quietly wins, and nobody trusts it. The agent-to-agent vision survives in a different form: an **open offer protocol between different brokerages**, piloted first with institutional sellers (Opendoor/Offerpad, builders) in year 2.
2. **Bot-to-human, not bot-to-bot.** The counterparty is a human listing agent with a phone. Our messages go out **under a named licensee, labeled AI-assisted,** by email or text.
3. **One moment, not the whole deal.** The wedge is the **Texas option period**: inspection report → priced ask list → three equal-value packages → TREC 39 amendment → read the counter → deadline clock. We don't touch search, showings or the offer in v1.
4. **The AI drafts, a human sends, only the principal signs.** Walk-away limits and priorities live in code, never in the prompt (research: limits written into prompts got conceded away; limits enforced in code held). Every message carries a non-binding footer. The AI has no signing credentials.
5. **North star: deals saved, not dollars squeezed.** Primary metric: **share of contracts that survive the option period** (vs. the agent's baseline). Second: **net value won for the buyer** (credits + price + repairs vs. the seller's first response). Guardrails: zero unapproved sends, zero missed option deadlines, zero reservation-price leaks.
6. **Working name: Punchlist.** "Option Day" is the launch campaign. Banned words: "robot," "replace your realtor."

## Business model: sequence it, don't pick blind
The team split. PM and the broker say **sell a tool to buyer agents** (legal-clean, 88% of buyers already have an agent). BizDev and the attorney say the only place real money (~$6K per deal) can legally sit is a **licensed brokerage** (Model A, Homa's structure). My call is to do them in order:

- **Phase 0–1 (months 0–6): "Option Desk" for buyer agents.** A per-file flat fee of **$249 per option-period package**, paid by the agent or team. The fee is fixed and not tied to closing, to stay clear of §1101.651. The licensee stays negotiator of record. First 20 files run as a **Wizard-of-Oz concierge** (a human negotiation expert + LLM drafting behind the scenes), with 10 Austin solo and small-team agents.
- **Gate at file 30:** do we show **≥$3K incremental net value** or **≥5 points better option-period survival** vs. a blind replay of the agents' past files?
- **Phase 2 (if the gate passes):** launch **Punchlist Realty**, our own licensed Austin buyer brokerage, with a Texas broker of record as co-founder. The pitch is **"1% back + negotiation guarantee"**: below $3K won after inspection, the rebate rises to 2%. Distribution comes from builders, relocation firms and a free **"Inspection → Ask List" tool** (coaching only, it never contacts the counterparty). RESPA-safe flat fees only with inspectors and lenders.
- **Option Desk continues** as a licensing line for brokerages outside Austin.

## Product v1 (Option Desk)
- Upload the inspection report (PDF) → an isolated parser (prompt-injection safe) → items with Austin repair-cost ranges. Specialist items (foundation, roof, sewer) get "get a bid" instead of a number.
- A buyer preference interview (5 minutes): what matters, walk-away, cash vs. repairs, timing.
- **Three equal-value packages** per counter. The seller's choice reveals their priorities.
- TREC 39 amendment generated by code from structured terms (the LLM never writes form text), with DocuSign.
- Counter reader: parse the listing agent's reply and propose the next move.
- Option clock with escalation to a human 48h, 24h and 6h before the deadline.
- A licensee approval queue, plus an audit log of every message.
- Cost: ~$10–40 inference per file. Build is 12 weeks with 3 engineers, *after* the concierge proves the value.

## Open tensions I want the team to stress-test
1. **Will buyer agents actually pay $249 a file** for something that implies "you're a weak negotiator"? Or does it have to be framed as "save your deal / save your time"?
2. **Is Phase 1 a real business or just a research step?** BizDev says B has low LTV and is copyable by KW Command/Compass. Should we skip straight to the brokerage?
3. **The listing agent's reaction.** Will AI-assisted counters get engaged with, ignored, or punished? Does disclosure hurt results?
4. **Liability.** Who is liable for a bad repair-cost estimate or a missed deadline: us, the licensee, or both? What E&O exists?
5. **Measurement.** How do we prove "incremental value" credibly per file when every house is different?
6. **Moat in 24 months:** outcome data on accepted asks? The protocol? The license plus brand?
7. **Does the agent-to-agent vision with iBuyers/builders deserve an earlier slot,** since that is where Omri's original idea actually fits (repeat, institutional, API-ready sellers)?
