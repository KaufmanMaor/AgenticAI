# Round 1: Broker view (veteran Austin TX broker, 15+ yrs)

**Bottom line:** The pain is real and the founder found it by instinct: deals die after the inspection, and they die over stupid stuff. But "my bot talks to your bot and they close it" misreads how the money moves and who holds the cards. The other side is a human listing agent on a cell phone 95%+ of the time. Build the best buyer-side *negotiation brain* in the business, put it under a license, and let a human carry it the last mile.

## 1. "Realtors negotiate badly": where Omri is right and wrong
- **Right about incentives.** On a $450K Austin house, cutting $10K off the price costs the buyer agent about $240 of commission (2.4%), and blowing the deal costs them about $10.8K. So we are paid to *close*, not to squeeze. Levitt & Syverson saw this from the seller side. Most agents push "just take the credit and let's close" because that's how they get paid.
- **Right that many are mediocre.** Texas has 150K+ licensees. Half do fewer than 5 deals a year, and plenty have never priced a foundation repair. They forward the inspection PDF and ask "what do you want to ask for?" That isn't negotiation.
- **Wrong that it's zero-sum price haggling.** Good agents already trade non-price terms constantly: closing date, leaseback, option days, title policy, survey, HOA transfer fees, repairs vs. credit vs. price cut. Win-win is our everyday job. The problem is *consistency*, not that the concept is missing.
- **Wrong about where the skill sits.** The real move is the "one more phone call." I call the listing agent at 8pm and say "off the record, what does your seller actually need?" and hear "she already bought in Round Rock, she needs to close by the 15th." That one call is worth $8K. It happens off-paper, it runs on a relationship, and it never shows up in an email thread.
- **Emotions are the actual battlefield.** The seller takes the inspection report as an insult to her home ("we raised three kids there"). The buyer is scared of the roof after watching YouTube. Half my job is *absorbing* emotion and translating it into terms. A bot that relays emotion faithfully makes things worse. A bot that strips emotion out completely misses the seller's real constraint.

## 2. Anatomy of an Austin resale deal, and where the money and blowups are
1. **Buyer rep agreement + showing.** Since 1/1/2026 (SB 1968) I need a written agreement before I show. Commission is negotiated here (~2.4-3%, or what the seller offers via concessions). *Money:* ~$11K on a $450K deal. *Blowup:* buyers balk at signing, and agents lose clients to rebate shops like Homa.
2. **Offer on TREC One to Four Family (20-18) + addenda:** Third Party Financing, Seller's Disclosure, HOA, Lender's Appraisal termination (49-x), and the Seller Financing addendum (rare). The negotiable blanks are price, Seller contribution (para 12), earnest money (~1%, $4-5K), option fee ($200-500 in today's Austin market), option days (5-10), closing date, title policy payer, survey/T-47. *Money:* in this buyer's market, $5-15K in concessions or rate buydown is routine. *Blowup:* low offers insult sellers, and the counter-war stalls over $3K.
3. **Option period (5-10 days).** Buyer can walk for any reason and loses only the option fee. **This is the high-leverage window.**
4. **Inspection ($450-800 plus specialty: foundation, sewer scope, HVAC).** Austin classics: foundation movement on clay soil (bids $8-25K), polybutylene or cast-iron drain lines ($6-20K), 20-year-old roof ($12-20K), aging HVAC ($8-14K), and electrical such as Federal Pacific panels ($2-4K).
5. **Amendment (TREC 39-x): repairs vs. credit vs. price reduction.** This is THE negotiation. Typical asks run $5-25K, and typical settlements land at 40-70% of the ask. Credit is capped by lender limits (often 3-6% IPC on conventional), and a price cut also affects appraisal and taxes. *Blowup:* most of Texas's 17-22% cancellations die right here, often over $1-3K, and often after the option clock has run to its last hour.
6. **Appraisal (week 2-3).** In flat or declining Austin, low appraisals of $5-20K are common. The gap gets split, paid in cash, or the buyer terminates under addendum 49. That's a second negotiation, and the bot could own it too.
7. **Financing / buyer approval.** Usually little to negotiate. Blowups come from DTI surprises, job changes, and rate-lock expiry. Extensions are negotiated via amendment.
8. **Title + survey.** Objection periods, encroachments, an HOA resale certificate. Small dollars ($500-3K), but they're deadline traps.
9. **Closing / funding.** Last-minute walkthrough disputes ($500-5K), and leaseback terms if the seller needs time.

**Where AI value is real:** steps 5 and 6. Turn the inspection into priced, prioritized asks (with real contractor bids), structure them the right way for the lender's limits, run scenarios, draft the amendment, and keep the deadline clock.

## 3. How a listing agent reacts to an AI counter
- **Default reaction: ignore it or get irritated.** If I get a text from a bot, my first move is to call the buyer's agent. If nobody human answers, I tell my seller "this buyer isn't serious" and work the backup offer. Listing agents control the seller's ear. Spook them and you lose.
- **What makes them ignore it:** no licensed human named on the IABS or contract, walls of text, "per my analysis," fake urgency, robotic persistence at 11pm, asks that ignore local norms (asking a seller to replace a 12-year-old roof), and anything that smells like it'll create liability for *them*.
- **What makes them engage:** it shows up as a clean, signed package from a licensed agent at a known brokerage: a one-page summary, contractor bids attached, a TREC amendment pre-filled, a "seller's choice" menu (repair X OR $Y credit OR $Z price cut), and fast answers. Listing agents are busy and lazy in the best sense. **Make their job easier and they'll love it, as long as they don't have to know it's a bot.** Disclosure is a question for the RE Attorney, but practically, the bot should be the buyer agent's *staff*, not a counterparty.

## 4. What AI can and can't do here
**Can do well:** read the inspection and price the repairs; pull comps and DOM to gauge seller leverage (106 days on market = motivated); model concessions against lender limits; draft TREC forms and amendments; track every deadline (option, financing, title objection); coach the buyer at 10pm when they're panicking; write tight, unemotional counters; run "what if they say no" trees; and enforce a hard floor and ceiling in code.
**Can't (or shouldn't) do:** showings and door access (lockbox access requires a license plus SentriLock); the relationship and "off the record" calls; reading a seller's voice tremor at the walkthrough; knowing that a certain builder's 2005 slab-on-grade homes in Pflugerville all heave; knowing which inspector is an alarmist; the reputation capital that makes a listing agent pick *my* client's offer among three; and legal accountability (TREC holds the broker liable).

## 5. First customer segment
**Not consumers going agent-less. Start with the agents.** Specifically:
1. **Solo / small-team buyer agents in Austin doing 6-25 deals a year.** They're overloaded with 3 deals in option at once, have no transaction coordinator for negotiation, and lose deals they shouldn't. They pay for anything that saves a deal ($11K at stake).
2. Second, **first-time and relocating buyers (tech transplants)** who already have an agent but want a "second brain." This is B2C upsell, but it creates friction with their agent.
3. Avoid listing-side and dual-agency ("intermediary") products at first. They're too sensitive.

## 6. Would I partner? What would I pay?
- **Yes, as a tool under my license.** I stay the agent of record and the bot is my assistant, with me approving every send.
- **Pricing I'd accept:** $99-199/month per agent, or **$150-300 per transaction** that reaches option-period negotiation. A success fee on a "deal saved" sounds nice, but it's impossible to attribute and agents hate it. A % of commission is a non-starter unless you're bringing me the leads.
- **The better partnership:** sell it to brokerages (Spyglass/Realty Austin/KW market centers) as a broker-level compliance + negotiation tool at $5-15K/year per office. Brokers care about fewer blown deals and fewer TREC complaints.
- **The founder's own licensed brokerage** (Homa-style) is the alternative, but then you're competing with every agent in town *and* need agents to carry the last mile.

## 7. Top 5 traps a tech founder will fall into
1. **Assuming the other side has an agent.** It's a human listing agent with a phone, and they'll bypass your bot to call a human. Build for bot-to-human, not bot-to-bot.
2. **Optimizing price when the goal is close rate.** A bot that "wins" $3K and kills 1 deal in 10 is a loser product. Measure deals saved and days-to-amendment.
3. **Unlicensed negotiation.** Texas Occ. Code 1101 makes it a Class A misdemeanor, and one TREC complaint and you're done. You need a licensed broker sponsor from day 1.
4. **Ignoring the deadline mechanics.** Option periods end at 5pm local time on a set day. Your bot can't "keep negotiating" past that, and a missed deadline costs the buyer their leverage. Form and clock bugs are malpractice.
5. **Thinking realtors are the enemy.** They control 88%+ of buyers and *all* listing agents. Positioning as "replacing bad realtors" gets you boycotted in every Facebook agent group in Austin. Position as "the negotiation assistant that saves your deals."

## 8. Questions for the other roles
- **PM:** Is the MVP an inspection-to-amendment copilot (agent-facing) or a consumer app? What's the one metric: cancellation rate, concessions won, or time saved?
- **Marketing:** How do we reach Austin agents (ABoR/Unlock MLS classes, brokerage meetings, agent FB groups)? What's the "saved my deal" story?
- **BizDev:** Which Austin brokerage would pilot? Can we partner with inspectors or contractor bid services (repair-pricing data is the moat)?
- **Tech:** Can we parse inspection reports from Spectora/HomeGauge reliably, price repairs locally, and enforce hard limits in code? How do we integrate with the forms software (ZipForms/Lone Wolf/Form Simplicity/DocuSign)?
- **RE Attorney:** Does AI-drafted counter text sent under my name count as "negotiation" by an unlicensed party? Must we disclose AI use to the other side? Who's liable for a bad recommendation: broker, vendor, or both? What does TREC say about AI under broker supervision rules (Rule 535.2)?
