# Round 1: Marketing / Growth view

*Role: Head of Marketing / Growth. US only, Austin first. This is an opinionated draft for debate. Sources: the research report and the notes in `research_notes/סוכני AI למשא ומתן בנדלן/`.*

## 1. Positioning: sell the moment, not the robot
Omri's vision is two bots closing deals between themselves. That is a 2029 story. Today it is unmarketable: only 7% of people would let AI negotiate for them, trust in AI for home search fell from 30% to 16%, and 88% of buyers have a human agent. So we do not sell "AI negotiates your home". We sell **the worst 7 days of buying a house in Texas**: the option period after the inspection report lands. 70% of agents blame this stage for failed deals, and $5-15K of Austin concessions get decided here.

**Positioning statement:** For Austin homebuyers who are under contract and holding an inspection report, [Name] turns a 60-page list of scary findings into a priced, prioritized ask and negotiates the repair-and-credit amendment with the seller's side. A licensed Texas broker approves every offer, and the limits you set are locked in code. Homa saves you money at the closing table. We get you money back *after the inspection*, when the deal is most likely to collapse.

**The single promise:** *"Get more back after the inspection, without blowing up the deal."*
Every message comes back to two numbers: **dollars recovered** and **deals saved**. We never claim to be "smarter than your realtor".

## 2. Overcoming the trust gap (from 7% to "why not?")
People don't trust AI to *decide*. They will trust it to *prepare, price and draft* if a named human signs. Trust has to be built into the product, not added in copy:
1. **Human in the loop, by name.** "Reviewed and sent by [Broker], TREC #xxxxx." A photo and phone number on every screen. Nothing binding goes out without a human tap from the buyer and the broker's sign-off. (Cotality: 75% want AI *with* a human in the loop. Give them exactly that.)
2. **"Your ceiling is locked in code."** Plain-English explainer: the AI that talks to the other side *cannot see* your walk-away number. A separate rules engine enforces it. Research shows prompt-only limits always leak, so we turn this weakness into our trust claim. Visual: a padlock on the max credit.
3. **Money-back guarantee.** If we don't recover at least **$1,500 more** than the seller's first response (or more than the buyer's own draft ask), the fee is $0. The success fee is a share of the *increment* only. Attorney and broker must approve this wording.
4. **Show the receipts.** Every user gets a negotiation log of who said what and why, plus a line-item repair price for each finding, sourced from local contractor quotes. Anonymized logs are published on the site ("Negotiation Replays").
5. **Public red-team bounty** (see 7): "Try to make our agent go past its limit. Win $1,000."
6. **AI disclosure up front.** 65% want AI use disclosed. We disclose to the other side as well, and say so proudly.

## 4. Messaging that avoids the "robot replaces your realtor" backlash
- **The agent is the hero.** "Works with your agent. Makes them look brilliant." For buyers who have an agent, we are the agent's back office: priced repair list, comps on credits, a drafted TREC amendment. Agent-facing line: *"Close the deals the inspection usually kills."*
- **For buyers:** "Your inspection report, priced line by line, in 1 hour." / "Ask for the right $8,400, not the wrong 78 repairs." / "Credits, not fights."
- **For listing agents** (the counterparty): "A clean, reasonable, ready-to-sign ask, so fewer terminations land on your desk." The listing side also wants the deal to close. That is our win-win.
- **Never:** "AI negotiator beats humans." DoNotPay ("robot lawyer") drew FTC action. Overclaiming is a legal risk, not just bad taste.

## 5. Beachhead audience in Austin (decision)
**Primary: buyers under contract, in or just before the option period, within roughly 72 hours of the inspection.** Intent, urgency and dollars at stake are all at their peak, and there is a hard deadline that forces a decision. It is the cheapest moment to reach people because they are already in the funnel of three known vendors: the **inspector**, the **lender** and the **title company**.
Sub-segments, ranked:
1. **Relocating tech workers** (Tesla, Apple, Oracle, Samsung Taylor, Dell). They don't know Texas forms or Austin contractors, they often buy remotely, they are AI-literate, and they arrive through relocation packages. Best early adopters.
2. **First-time buyers.** The most anxious, so the "don't blow it up" message works best. Lower prices, so smaller credits.
3. **Self-represented buyers buying new or resale** (~9-12%). We negotiate end-to-end through our broker. Clean licensing story, small segment.
- **Not FSBO sellers.** At 5% of sales, FSBO is a niche and Ridley's territory. Seller-side "listing defense" comes in phase 2.

## 6. CAC reality and cheaper wedges
Portal leads in real estate cost about **$7.4K per closed deal** (Zillow at about 3% conversion). Referral networks take 25-40% of commission. Against a $1-3K negotiation fee, buying leads loses money every time. **Rule: we never buy portal leads.** Wedges, from cheapest:
1. **Free "Inspection Report Pricer."** Upload a PDF and get line-item repair costs plus a "what's worth asking for" score within an hour. It gets organic traffic from searches like "Austin inspection report what to ask for" and from r/Austin and r/FirstTimeHomeBuyer. Target: **<$40 per upload**, and **15%** of uploads that are under contract converting to paid.
2. **Inspector partnerships** (Austin has hundreds of TREC-licensed inspectors). A co-branded "price this report" button in their delivery email. The value to them is better reviews and fewer angry 2am calls. **No referral payments until the RE attorney clears RESPA and TREC rules.**
3. **Lenders and title companies.** A saved deal is a saved loan and a saved title policy. Co-marketing, no pay-per-lead.
4. **Relocation HR and ERGs.** Lunch-and-learns like "Buying in Texas 101", plus Blind and Slack communities.
5. **Agents as distribution.** Free copilot seats for 20 Austin buyer agents. Each one brings 1-3 deals a month.
Target blended CAC by day 90: **< $500 per paid negotiation**.

## 7. Launch stunts
- **"Break the Ceiling" bounty.** A public sandbox where anyone tries to talk our agent past its locked limit, with $1,000 per verified breach. It is a nod to the $1 Chevy Tahoe chatbot story. It shows the code-enforced floor and earns tech press (HN, The Verge). If the agent breaches, we fix it publicly. Honest either way.
- **Austin Repair Price Index.** A monthly public report, from our uploads, of what foundation, roof and HVAC findings really cost and what sellers actually concede. Local TV, the Austin American-Statesman and Axios Austin love local data. This is our PR engine.
- **"Option Day" hotline.** A free 15-minute call with our broker during your option period, running for 30 days. Every call is a lead with a deadline.
- **Blind benchmark.** 50 real anonymized Austin inspection reports. Our recommended ask versus asks drafted by experienced agents, judged by retired listing agents. Publish the full method. Framing: "AI + agent vs. agent alone", never "AI vs. realtor".

## 9. Risks
- **Realtor backlash.** Austin's Unlock MLS and the board can make life hard: listing agents might refuse our amendments or "ghost" us. Mitigation: agent-hero messaging, our own licensed broker on every ask, and the listing-side benefit (fewer terminations).
- **NAR and TREC.** NAR's 2026 policy says agentic AI may not perform licensed activities. TREC advertising rules require the broker name, the IABS notice and no misleading claims. "AI negotiates" ad copy could itself be a violation. Every line goes through legal review.
- **FTC "AI washing".** Every claim about savings must be substantiated, so we publish the method.
- **Homa copy risk.** Homa is already in Austin with a 2% rebate and could add an "inspection negotiation" feature in a quarter. Our moat is to own the post-inspection *data* (the Price Index) and the inspector channel first.
- **A single bad story** ("AI agent cost me my dream home") kills trust in a 16%-trust market. That is why the human sign-off and the guarantee are non-negotiable.
