# Round 1: Head of BizDev on who pays, the unit economics, and the path to $1M

**Bottom line:** we should be a **licensed Austin buyer brokerage (Model A)**. Its AI runs the option-period renegotiation, and a human broker signs off on every commitment. Partnerships (D) are how we acquire customers, not a separate business. The copilot (B) is a **year-2 licensing line** once we have outcome data. The consumer success fee (C) becomes a **pricing feature inside A**, not a standalone company. Texas law effectively makes this choice for us. Negotiating for someone else for pay is brokerage under §1101.002, and §1101.651 bars a broker from paying an unlicensed company for broker acts. So the only place a deal-linked fee can land is inside a licensed entity or as SaaS sold to licensees. Of those two, only A captures about $6K per deal rather than about $200.

All figures below are my assumptions unless the research notes cite them. Base case: an Austin home at **$430K** (metro median ~$412-450K in 2026), buyer commission **2.4%** (Redfin Q3 2025: 2.42%), so the gross buyer side is **$10,320**.

## 2. Model A unit economics (one Austin deal at $430K)

| Line | $ | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Gross buyer commission (2.4%) | 10,320 | ~10-15% of deals end below 2.4%, so blended is ~$9.8K |
| Buyer rebate (1.0%) | -4,300 | Homa rebates ~2% (about $8,600) and keeps ~1% |
| **Net revenue** | **6,020** | |
| Licensed agent of record (salaried at ~40 deals a year) | -1,800 | Human approval on every counter. Required by TREC and the NAR AI policy |
| Broker of record, E&O, MLS/ABoR dues, compliance | -400 | |
| Inference, AI transaction coordinator, repair-pricing quotes | -250 | e.g. TheQwikFix-style priced repair quotes |
| **Gross profit** | **3,570 (59%)** | |
| CAC (blended) | -2,500 | Must fall to ≤$1.5K through D |
| **Contribution** | **~1,070** | ~$2,000 at partner-heavy CAC |

**Why a 1% rebate beats Homa's 2%:** we don't win a rebate war against a funded competitor. Our pitch is **"1% back plus a negotiation guarantee."** If we don't win at least $3K in repair credits or a price cut after inspection, we raise the rebate to 2%. The guarantee costs ~$4.3K per miss. At an assumed 25% miss rate, the expected cost is ~$1.1K per deal, which takes contribution to roughly break-even at blended CAC. So the guarantee is only affordable once CAC is partner-heavy (~$1.5K). That makes D the priority, not a nice-to-have. It is also how C lives on: as an outcome-based rebate, legal through the brokerage. Concessions of $5-15K are common in Austin (unverified broker sources) and ~46% of US sales include them. If the AI adds even $3-5K over a typical agent, the buyer comes out roughly level with Homa and has the evidence in writing. **This is the bet: if we can't show ≥$3K in incremental value per deal by deal 30, Model A fails.**

## 3. Models B and C: why they're not the company

- **B math:** $1M ARR = **~560 seats at $149/mo**. That's ~3% of Austin's ~17K+ Realtors (ABoR size unverified), or a statewide roll-out. Agent SaaS churns ~4-5% a month. At ~20 months of life, LTV ≈ $2.5K against $600-1,500 CAC, so LTV/CAC is 2-4x at best. It's a feature that incumbents can copy, so we don't lead with it. We sell it in year 2 to teams and independent brokerages outside Austin, priced **per option-period package ($199)**, not per seat.
- **C math:** gross $1.2K per deal, and the partner broker keeps ~30%, leaving ~$840. The segment is small, attribution of "savings" will be disputed, and the regulatory exposure is the worst of the four. We keep the **free "Inspection → Ask List" tool** as top-of-funnel. It coaches only and doesn't contact the counterparty. Anyone who uses it gets offered a Model A buyer agreement.

## 5. What it takes to reach $1M (Model A, about 15-18 months after launch)

- **~170 closings a year at $6K net ≈ 14 a month.** At ~85% contract-to-close (Austin cancels at ~14%), that means ~17 contracts a month. At ~25% of signed buyers reaching contract within 6 months, it means **~65 new buyer agreements a month**, from ~430 qualified leads a month at 15% conversion.
- **Team:** 1 Texas broker of record (ideally a co-founder), 4-5 salaried licensed agents at ~40 deals each (2x Redfin's 22 per agent, because the AI does the TC and drafting work), and 1 ops/TC.
- **Channel mix target:** 40% partners, 30% organic (the free inspection tool and Austin SEO content), 30% paid. **Cash need:** ~$425K CAC + ~$650K payroll, so roughly **$1.2-1.5M of seed runway** to reach a run-rate of $1M.
- **Milestones:** 10 closed deals with documented credits ≥$3K over baseline by month 6, then 50 closed deals and a blended CAC ≤$1.8K by month 12.

## 6. Partner targets (named, Austin-first)

- **Broker of record / sponsor (if no co-founder broker):** independent Austin brokerages such as **Spyglass Realty** and Austin **Keller Williams** market centers (Final Offer launched in Texas through KW, which is a precedent). The fallback is a flat-fee sponsoring broker (e.g. a Fathom- or Real-style cloud brokerage) at ~$500 per deal.
- **Builders (the co-op commission is already paid, and they don't fight buyer agents):** **Lennar, D.R. Horton, Taylor Morrison, Perry Homes, Pulte, KB Home, Meritage** in Hays and Williamson counties. Our pitch is that our AI shortens time-to-contract on aging spec inventory, where buydowns are already common.
- **Relocation (high-intent buyers on a deadline):** **Cartus, SIRVA/BGRS, Aires, Weichert Workforce Mobility**, which can open doors to Austin's big relocating employers (**Tesla Giga Texas, Samsung Taylor, Apple, Oracle, Dell**). Expect a 30-35% referral fee, which is acceptable because it replaces CAC.
- **Inspection (the moment of truth):** **Spectora** and **Porch/ISN** (inspector software, embedded at the report stage), **HomeGauge**, top local Austin inspectors, and **TheQwikFix** (repair pricing). This needs a **RESPA-safe structure**: a flat software or marketing fee, never per-referral.
- **Lenders:** **PrimeLending, Guild, Better**, and Austin credit unions **UFCU and A+FCU**. Rebate approval and co-marketing only, under RESPA §8 constraints.
- **iBuyers as the first true AI-to-AI counterparty:** **Opendoor** (Key Agent) and **Offerpad** (HENRY) resell Austin inventory with algorithmic pricing. They're institutional, repeat sellers, which is the Pactum/Walmart pattern. That makes them the most realistic place to pilot the founder's agent-to-agent vision, through an API negotiation pilot in year 1 or 2. **Indigo and Final Offer** are the listing-side protocol partners.

## 7. Questions for the other roles

1. **PM:** can we scope the MVP to option-period renegotiation only (inspection report → priced ask list → amendment draft → agent approval), and define the "baseline credit" we'd guarantee against in a way that's measurable per deal?
2. **Marketing:** can "1% back plus a negotiation guarantee" beat "2% back" (Homa) in a landing-page test? And can the free inspection tool produce ~430 qualified leads a month in Austin at under $50 each?
3. **Tech:** what does it cost to build a repair-pricing engine with ±20% accuracy on Austin inspection items, and what are the code-enforced floors and approval gates per counter?
4. **Texas Broker:** is 40 deals a year per salaried agent realistic when AI drafts the TREC amendments? How do Austin listing agents react to AI-written counters? And does a guaranteed or variable rebate raise TREC advertising issues?
5. **RE Attorney:** (a) is an outcome-contingent rebate permitted under TREC rules and lender interested-party-contribution caps? (b) Does the inspection tool cross into "negotiating for another" if it drafts the counter? (c) Which RESPA §8 structures with inspectors and lenders are safe? (d) What intermediary consent would we need for a future iBuyer API pilot if we ever represent both sides?
