# Round 3: BizDev sign-off on Concept v3

**(a) SIGN-OFF WITH CONDITIONS.** v3 is legally clean and cheap to kill at week 13 (about $150K at risk). But Option Desk alone is a good business, not a venture-scale one, and the gate as written measures how aggressive the ask is, not what the buyer actually won.

**(b) Biggest remaining risk: buyer agents won't pay for, or keep using, software on the part of the job they see as their value.** Rulings 3, 4 and 8(c) reframe the product and gate on it, but nothing tests it before the build. **Cheapest test (2 weeks, ~$0):** at weeks 2-5, pre-sell a **refundable 10-file pack at $1,490** to the agents we recruit. If fewer than 5 of 15 pay up front, stop before running the concierge.

**(c) Inconsistencies.**
1. **Option Desk alone tops out at roughly $10-40M ARR at $249 per file.** All figures are rough:
   - **Texas:** 335K sales, ~88% represented, ~80% keep the option or inspection step, plus ~15% of files that later cancel. That's ~250K files a year. 100% penetration would be ~$62M ARR. A realistic 10-20% share is **$6-12M ARR**.
   - **US:** ~4.06M sales on the same ratios gives ~3.2M inspection negotiations, so ~$800M at 100%. But other states use different forms and processes, so this needs rework state by state. A 5% share is **~$40M ARR**.
   - **Venture scale needs $100M ARR,** which means ~400K paid files, over 12% of every inspection negotiation in the US, with team-pack discounts pulling the average price down. That's implausible from per-file fees alone.
   - The $3-4M seed story therefore needs a named expansion path: appraisal-gap and listing-side files, enterprise licences for brokerages ($5-15K per office), a priced-outcome data product sold to iBuyers, lenders and insurers, and the deferred brokerage. **Write that path into v3 before the raise.**
2. **Gate (a) compares our ask with the agent's sealed draft ask.** That rewards asking for more, which contradicts the "deals saved" north star and the decision to drop the money guarantee. **Fix:** gate on the *accepted* outcome against the agent's own plan, judged by the blind panel, and keep the ask-vs-ask comparison as a secondary metric. Also add a guardrail: in no more than 1 of 30 files did our ask trigger a termination.
3. **The numbers for the volume plan don't add up.** 30 live files in 8 weeks from 10 agents means about 3 option periods per agent. An agent doing 15 deals a year sees only ~2.3 in that window. **Recruit 15-20 agents.**
4. **"Billed to the sponsoring broker" conflicts with the buyer.** We sell to solo and small-team agents, but under ruling 3 their *broker* must pay. That makes each sale a slower broker-level sale (a KW market-center style approval). Confirm in the attorney memo whether a licensee can pay from their own funds. If not, plan for broker-level sales from week 2.
5. **"Brokerage in a different metro, separate brand"** works only for a while. Once Option Desk spreads across Texas, every metro is a customer metro. **Decide A vs B for good at month 12. Don't defer it indefinitely.**

**Verdict: YES.** It's worth a founder's next 12 months, because a 13-week, ~$150K test settles the one question that matters (will Texas agents pay for AI-run option-period negotiation that measurably saves deals?), and a pass opens a defensible data asset plus an easy path to a seed round.
