# Round 3: Marketing / Growth sign-off on Concept v3

**(a) SIGN-OFF WITH CONDITIONS.** The positioning is right: a "negotiation transaction coordinator," white-labeled, with a process guarantee and the disclosure up front. But the pilot design can't yet tell us whether agents will pay, which is the one thing marketing has to prove.
- Conditions: (1) fix the pricing and gate inconsistency below before recruiting starts; (2) a 24/7 or evening SLA, not "4 business hours"; (3) there is one decision-maker for the purchase, the agent, with broker approval only as a one-time checkbox.

**(b) Biggest remaining risk: willingness to pay *repeatedly*.** The pain is episodic. A 12-deal agent hits an option period about once a month, so a habit never forms. Churn looks like silence, not cancellation. LTV per agent is about $1.5K a year at $149 a file, and that only works with near-zero CAC.
- **Cheapest test (2 weeks, about $0):** run the "Bring us a dead deal" replay for 20 agents, then ask each one to **prepay a 3-file pack at $399 on a card today**, refundable if unused.
- **Pass:** 6 or more of 20 prepay. **Kill signal:** fewer than 3.
- This measures cash intent before the concierge burns 8 weeks.

**(c) Inconsistencies in v3**
1. **Free files swallow the pay gate.** Two free files per agent and about 30 files across 10 agents in 8 weeks means most agents reach at most one paid file. Gate (c), "≥60% pay for a further file," is barely observable. Fix: 1 free file, or the prepay test above.
2. **Payer ≠ buyer.** Billing goes "to the sponsoring broker," yet we recruit solo agents and the kill criterion counts "agents who pay." Pick one. I'd say the agent pays, and the broker approves once.
3. **SLA vs. pitch.** The framing promises "save your night," but the guarantee is "4 *business* hours." A report that lands at 9pm on day 3 of a 5-day option is exactly when we would be silent.
4. **The gate rewards nitpicking.** Gate (a) measures dollars over a *larger ask*, which ruling 5 rejects as a goal. The sealed-draft baseline also forces the agent to do the work we claim to remove, so pilot friction will understate adoption. Measure *accepted* value (signed amendment vs. sealed draft), and limit sealed drafts to a subset of files.
5. **Data rights vs. confidentiality.** "Every contract grants anonymized data rights," but the data belongs to the agent's buyer client, under the TREC confidentiality duty. That needs buyer consent in the AI addendum, or the moat in ruling 14 is legally hollow.
6. **Copy drift.** My Round 2 DM promised "2 hours"; the v3 commitment is 4. All outreach must use one number.

**Verdict:** **Yes, as a hard-gated 90-day bet that earns the remaining 9 months, not a blind 12-month commitment.** The pain is real, the channel is reachable and the legal shape is clean, but the agent-SaaS ceiling is low, so the venture case rests on the outcome-data moat and a later brokerage or iBuyer step.
