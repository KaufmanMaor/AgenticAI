# Round 3: RE attorney sign-off on Concept v3
*This is internal ideation input, not legal advice. Items marked [U] are unverified and need confirmation in the ~$3K counsel memo.*

**(a) SIGN-OFF WITH CONDITIONS.** v3 makes the right calls on all four core risks:
- Buyer side only, bot-to-human.
- The AI drafts, a human sends, and the principal signs.
- A fixed fee billed to the broker.
- No outcome guarantee and no consumer tool.

What is left is a short list of drafting and structuring fixes. None of them is a blocker.

**(b) Biggest remaining risk.** Once our company holds a TREC license and its licensees shape strategy on another broker's file, TREC or a buyer could treat us as providing brokerage services to that buyer. In that case:
- §1101.563 would require **our own** written agreement with the buyer, which we don't have.
- Texas no longer recognises subagency (IABS 1-2, 2026), so there is no role we can lawfully fill on the buyer's side.
- Our errors would become license-discipline matters.

**Cheapest fix.** Ask the ~$3K memo one specific question, and build the contracts around the answer:
1. A broker-to-broker **services agreement** stating that we serve the sponsoring broker, represent no party, and owe no duties to the parties.
2. Our staff have zero contact with principals or the counterparty.
3. All advice reaches the buyer only through the licensee's judgment.
4. Optionally, an informal question to TREC staff.

**(c) Inconsistencies in v3:**
- **Holding a license while selling B2B makes §1101.651 easier, not harder.** Payments between license holders are permitted. The cost is that we now fall under TREC jurisdiction:
  - Our B2B marketing may count as license-holder advertising under 22 TAC §535.155, so it needs our broker name.
  - Consumer complaints about Option Desk become complaints against our license.
  - Any concierge licensee who is a sales agent may be paid only by our company.
- **The disclosure footer (ruling 7) dropped two items from Round 2:** the sending agent's **brokerage name** and "who represents the Buyer". Restore both.
- **"White-label to the agent" conflicts with a footer that names Option Desk.** Either is fine, but the label "AI-assisted" must never be removed.
- **The anonymized data rights (ruling 14) belong to the buyer, not the agent.** It is the buyer's confidential information, so the consent has to come from the buyer through the AI/vendor consent addendum. Also:
  - "Anonymized" needs a defined standard; addresses plus prices re-identify easily.
  - The contracts must bar any use of the data by a future Punchlist Realty, to match ruling 1's firewall.
- **The pre-drafted Notice of Termination (ruling 10) needs a hard rule:** it is never sent without the buyer's documented instruction, and the licensee sends it, never the system.
- **The designated broker's 10–15% equity will likely dilute below 10% at seed.** That may trigger the mandatory $1M business-entity E&O under §1101.355 [U]. Budget for it now, since ruling 12 already plans to buy it.
- **"File free if the ask list is late" is fine.** It prices our own service level, not the deal outcome. Keep its wording off any closing or credit metric.

**Verdict: YES.** The legal path is now clear, cheap to confirm (about $3K and 2 weeks), and has hard kill criteria. Over the next 12 months the real risk is commercial (will agents pay and send our drafts?), not legal, and the pilot is designed to answer exactly that.
