# Round 1 — Marketing / Growth lens

*Role: VP Marketing (B2B2C, community-led growth, Israeli tech). Opinionated draft for discussion.*

## 1. Positioning: kill the dating frame, keep the dating mechanics

"Hooked for jobs" is a good **origin story** and a bad **category**. Keep the mechanics (event-scoped, QR in, data deleted at midnight). Drop the language: no "match", "swipe" or "date" anywhere in the product.

- **HR buyers:** "dating" means legal risk (harassment, unstructured interviews, discrimination) and looks unserious. The buyer is a Head of TA who has to justify ₪2,500 to a CFO. "Speed InterviewDate" would get the deck closed.
- **Candidates:** dating means being exposed and judged on looks. The opposite of what an employed engineer standing next to their manager wants.
- **Press:** the Hooked lineage is worth one launch headline. Use it once, then leave it.

**Category I'd own:** *the hiring layer for professional events*. Consumer tagline: **"The hallway track, with intent."** Every Israeli developer knows the best part of Reversim is the hallway. We make it purposeful.

**Positioning statement:** For tech recruiters who need passive, senior talent, [Name] turns any professional conference into a private hiring floor: verified people in your industry, in the room today, meeting your hiring manager over coffee. No job-board spam, no AI filter, no fake candidates.

I disagree with the research on one point. It treats candidates as the free side and an afterthought. In the 2026 Israeli market juniors are plentiful and seniors are scarce. If we don't win **senior passive** candidates, recruiters will see a flood of juniors, churn, and we turn into a job fair. So the candidate brand matters as much as the sales deck, even though candidates never pay.

## 3. Messaging by audience

**Candidates** (passive, employed, cynical about recruiters)
- "Your boss won't see you. The hiring managers in this room will."
- "No CV filter. No ATS black hole. 15 minutes, face to face."
- "Everything is deleted at midnight." (Borrowed from Hooked. This is the trust hook.)
- Status options are **"Curious / Open to talk / Actively looking"**, never "job seeker". "Curious" is the default and gives plausible deniability.

**Recruiters / Heads of TA**
- "Verified people. Real badges. Zero fake candidates." (Gartner's fraud stat and the 74% shift back to in-person interviews are our best sales line.)
- "Eight coffees with senior engineers instead of 800 AI-generated applications."
- Pricing anchored to lead retrieval: "Costs less than one day of agency fee."

**Organizers**
- "A new line on your sponsor form, with 25% revenue share and zero ops."
- A "Talent Partner" sponsor tier. Their recruiters get the hiring badge, and their own employees are hidden by default. This answers the "you're poaching my sponsors' people" objection.

## 4. Go-to-market: first 3 Israeli events (concierge, no app)

Target **dev-community conferences, not mega-events.** Cyber Week (Nov 9–12, 4.5k–11k attendees, a third from abroad, policy-heavy) is too diffuse for a first test. A tight run, with dates taken from aggregators and still to be confirmed:

1. **Reversim Summit** (~Nov 30–Dec 1): the community gold standard for senior engineers.
2. **AI Dev TLV** (~Dec 21): the hottest hiring category.
3. **DevOpsDays TLV** (~Dec 22): a scarce-skill niche, and it falls the next day, so we reuse the setup.

**Rules:**
- **No go without supply.** We only run an event with ≥5 hiring companies and ≥15 open roles pre-sold. Founding price ₪1,500–2,500, refunded if there are fewer than 5 meetings. An empty screen is the death moment, so we never ship one.
- **Hiring managers, not only HR.** Each paying company must bring an engineering lead for at least 3 hours. Israeli devs will skip a recruiter and sit down with a VP R&D.
- **Most activation happens before the event.** The organizer's T-72h email says "14 companies are hiring at Reversim. See roles, book a 15-min coffee." The founder's "see who's here right now" is the backup. The pre-booked slot is the product.
- Founders run matching by hand on WhatsApp, with one physical **"Hallway Coffee" corner** (sponsor-funded espresso bar, 3 tables, out of sight lines from the expo floor).

## 5. On-site activation

- **QR on the back of the badge** plus a table tent at the coffee corner. The entrance QR is wasted: people are busy registering.
- **A 20-second stage mention before the 10:30 break**, read by the MC rather than us: "12 companies are hiring today. Coffee's on them."
- **Recruiters are visible, candidates aren't.** Hiring staff wear a bright "Hiring — ask me" lanyard. Candidates wear nothing.
- **Gamify recruiters, never candidates.** A live screen shows "Fastest reply" and "Most coffees" per company. Points for candidates would expose them.
- **Free coffee per meeting**, which doubles as a cover story.

## 6. Viral loops and content

- **Recruiter FOMO is the main loop.** "Wix, monday and 10 others are hiring at DevOpsDays. You aren't." Talent teams copy each other.
- **"Hired in the Hallway" stories, told by the employer, not the candidate**, posted 90 days after the person starts and with their consent. Candidates won't post. Companies love to.
- **Event Hiring Report** for each conference (companies, meetings, roles, median reply time). The organizer co-brands it, and it's a ready data story for Geektime/Calcalist. It also gives us real numbers to replace the made-up "60%".
- **Referral:** "Not for me, but my friend fits." The one loop candidates can share without risk.

## 7. Two bold / contrarian ideas

1. **Flip the visibility.** I go further than the research's "only recruiters see candidates". **Candidates never declare anything.** The app is a live map of *who's hiring in this room*. Candidates browse anonymously and knock, and their identity is revealed only when the recruiter accepts. This removes the discretion problem completely and makes the screen never-empty, because we pre-sold the supply. Recruiter outbound search comes later as an add-on, only for candidates who opt in.
2. **No app at all for the first year.** In Israel, WhatsApp *is* the app: badge QR, then a WhatsApp bot, then a calendar slot. That means zero download friction. Build the app only once 10 events prove demand. And put the "InterviewDate booth" off until later, then bring it back once as a PR stunt: the **"Boomerang Booth"** at event 3, where Israelis in the room talk live to an Israeli-founded company's team in NYC. It earns headlines, costs one laptop, and keeps the remote booth out of the core product.

## 8. Open questions for other roles

1. **HR / PM:** Does "Curious, browse and knock" turn into real interviews, or do passive seniors only browse? What first-meeting format (15 minutes structured? a coffee chat?) is defensible on discrimination and still useful to a hiring manager?
2. **Bizdev:** Will organizers like Reversim (community, non-profit feel) and commercial ones (Calcalist, Geektime) accept a recruiting layer next to paying sponsors? Is "sponsor employees hidden by default" enough, or do sponsors demand exclusivity?
3. **Tech:** Can we hide a person's own employer reliably using only the badge's registration data (company field, email domain), without LinkedIn? And can a WhatsApp-only MVP keep the "deleted at midnight" promise in a way we could show an auditor?
