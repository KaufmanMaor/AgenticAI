# Round 1 — Head of BizDev: who pays, how we sell it, how it grows

**Bottom line:** recruiters pay, organizers sell for us, and sponsors buy the premium tier. Candidates never pay. We're selling "lead retrieval for talent," with a price per event that recruiters can line up against a cost per hire.

## 1. Who pays and why

| Party | Pays? | Why they'd care |
|---|---|---|
| **Recruiters / in-house TA** | **Yes, primary** | A face-to-face, verified, pre-filtered candidate costs less than a LinkedIn seat or an agency fee (15-25% of salary, which is about ₪60-100K for a senior dev). Fake AI-generated applicants make in-person meetings worth more every year. |
| **Organizers** | They receive money, they don't pay | New non-ticket revenue (a 25% rev share) plus a "career value" story that sells more tickets. They're our sales channel. |
| **Sponsors** | Yes, premium | A "Hiring Partner" upgrade to their sponsorship package (see §5). |
| **Candidates** | No | Charging them kills liquidity, and the passive senior engineer we're after won't pay. At most a free "boost" later. |

## 2. Pricing options

| Model | Pro | Con | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| Per-event **Talent Pass** ($600-1,200) | Matches the lead-retrieval budget line, simple to buy, no attribution fights | Revenue is lumpy, and value depends on what happens on the day | **Launch model** |
| Per-hire success fee (8-10% of salary) | Big ticket, easy to justify | Attribution is unprovable 60-90 days later and the sales cycle is long. Puts us up against agencies | Optional add-on in year 2 |
| Annual subscription ("All-Access", ~$6-9K/yr for unlimited Israeli events) | Recurring and predictable, priced below a LinkedIn seat | Needs a calendar of 15+ events first | **Year 2 core** |
| Organizer license (SaaS, $3-8K/event) | One buyer, fast | Same game Brella and Swapcard already play. Organizer has no hiring pain to solve | Only for white-label deals |
| Sponsored lounge / booth ($8-20K) | High margin, very visible | Can only be sold at big events | **Upsell from day 1** |

**Recommendation:** Talent Pass plus a sponsored Hiring Lounge in year 1. Move repeat buyers to an annual subscription in year 2. Keep the success fee only for the remote kiosk, where attribution is clean.

## 3. Unit economics: one Israeli tech conference with 1,500 attendees (all assumptions)

- Attendees who scan the QR: 35% = **525**. Of those, 20% mark "open" (only recruiters can see them) = **~105 candidates**.
- Recruiting companies: **15 Talent Passes at an average of $900** = $13,500.
- **1 Hiring Lounge sponsor** = $10,000.
- **Gross revenue: $23,500**
- Organizer rev share, 25% of passes only: -$3,400
- On-site ops (2 staff, lounge setup, signage, interview corners): -$3,000
- Allocated sales cost (about 1 BD-week per event): -$2,500
- **Contribution: about $14.5K per event (~62%)**
- What the recruiter gets: about 6 meetings per pass, 90 in total, leading to an assumed **3-5 hires within 90 days**. That's about **$2.7-4.5K per hire for all recruiters combined**, against an industry cost per hire of $4-5K and agency fees of $25K+. It's a win, but only just. **The density threshold matters most: fewer than 60 open candidates and the recruiter won't renew.**

## 4. Organizer partnerships: who to approach first

**Tier A: beachhead (Q1-Q2). Dev and engineering audiences, where hiring pain is highest:**
- **Reversim Summit:** run by the community, with a self-reported attendance of "thousands" of developers, PMs and data scientists. Approach it as a community partner: no license fee, a donation to the community, and a free pilot.
- **DevOpsDays Tel Aviv:** in-person only. The 2025 edition was at Expo TLV, and devopsdays.org lists the 2026 edition for **Dec 22, 2026**. This is a realistic first pilot.
- **Geektime Code:** about **1,500 developers** (2024 edition). A commercial organizer with a sponsor-sales team, so the rev-share pitch fits.
- **GopherCon Israel** (Oct 18, 2026), **AI Dev TLV** (Dec 21, 2026), **AWS Summit TLV:** smaller events where we can practise the concierge MVP.

**Tier B: scale (H2). Large flagship events:**
- **Cyber Week (TAU):** **4,500 participants** in Dec 2025, and the 2026 edition moved to **Nov 9-12, 2026**. Strong on security hiring, though attendees skew toward government.
- **AI Week (TAU):** a third-party listing gives 5,000-8,000 attendees. Unverified, and its dates may overlap with Cyber Week.
- **Cybertech Global TLV** (Jan 26-28, 2026 at Expo TLV): heavy on executives and government, so it suits a **Hiring Lounge sponsor deal** rather than mass candidate traffic.
- **Calcalist and Geektime business conferences:** not a fit for candidates. Use them for brand and to sell the annual subscription to TA leaders.
- **Meetups** (Google for Startups, Wix Engineering, AWS user groups): no revenue, but they're where we run product reps cheaply. Offer them free.

**How to approach organizers:** never open with "an app for your attendees." Open with: **"We bring you 15 new paying customers (recruiters) who aren't your sponsors today. You get 25%. We run it on-site. No integration needed."** Then: a free pilot at the first event, a case study, and a paid rev share from the second event on. **WeAreDevelopers** (Berlin, about 15K devs) is the first global target once we have 3 Israeli case studies.

## 5. The sponsor poaching conflict, turned into a feature

The fear is that a sponsor pays ₪100K for a booth and a competitor poaches its engineers at the same event. Four ways to defuse it:
1. **Employer Shield (on by default, for everyone):** a candidate is never shown to recruiters from their current employer or its group. This protects candidates too.
2. **Hiring Partner tier, sold only to sponsors:** 24-hour early access to the candidate pool, a branded lounge, and first choice of interview slots. Non-sponsor pass holders get access only from day 2, or the organizer can cap their number at around 10.
3. **Sponsor-only mode as an organizer option:** if the organizer prefers, only sponsors can buy passes. That makes the app a **reason to upgrade a sponsorship**, not a threat to it.
4. **Mutual consent:** a candidate's details are revealed only after both sides accept. So no one is "poached" without choosing to be.

## 6. Event platforms: integrate, compete, or get acquired

- **Integrate** with Bizzabo (Israeli-founded), Swapcard, Brella and Grip through their marketplaces and APIs: attendee SSO and a "Hiring" tab. That way their organizer customers become our distribution.
- **Don't compete** on general networking. Our moat is the **recruiter side**: a network of paying TA buyers and links to ATS tools (Comeet, Greenhouse, Ashby). The platforms don't have this.
- **Exit thesis:** in 3-4 years we're a logical **acquisition target** for an event platform (to add a revenue line), an ATS or job board (LinkedIn, Indeed, or Handshake's events arm), or Hooked itself (see Bold idea #2).

## 7. Revenue path (assumptions)

| | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Events | 12 (8 Israel pilots, 4 paid at full price) | 40 (25 Israel, 15 EU/US) | 120 (incl. white-label) |
| Average revenue per event | $8K (heavy discounting at first) | $18K | $20K |
| Event revenue | ~$100K | ~$720K | ~$2.4M |
| Annual subscriptions | 0 | 30 × $7K = $210K | 120 × $8K = $960K |
| Platform / white-label licenses | 0 | ~$50K | ~$400K |
| **Total** | **~$100K** | **~$1M** | **~$3.8M** |

The key milestone for year 1 isn't revenue. It's **3 hires we can document and over 50% of recruiters buying again.**

## 8. Two bold ideas

1. **Remote Recruiter Pass / "Speed InterviewDate" kiosk as a cross-border product.** US and European companies with Israeli R&D, plus relocation-friendly employers, pay **$2,500 per event plus a $3K success fee** to run video interviews from a kiosk at the venue without flying in. Attribution is clean and the margin is high. It also turns Israeli conferences into a global sourcing channel. The kiosk can be launched as a branded product sponsored by a single global employer.
2. **A joint venture or license with Hooked ("Hooked Pro").** Hooked already has the QR entry, the "who's here now" engine, ephemeral data handling, organizer relationships and about 15K downloads. Pitch them on a revenue split on a B2B line they would never build alone. We bring the recruiter sales team and they bring the technology. That saves us 6-9 months and gives us a story an acquirer will like.

## 9. Open questions for the other roles

1. **Product / UX:** how do we detect someone's current employer reliably for the Employer Shield (self-declared, email domain, LinkedIn) without making the 2-minute onboarding longer? And what does a candidate see when only 3 recruiters are present?
2. **Growth / marketing:** what activation tactics (organizer push, badge QR, a talk from the stage) can get **35% of attendees to scan**? And can we promise organizers at least 60 open candidates before they sign?
3. **Legal / finance:** under Israel's Privacy Protection Law Amendment 13 (effective Aug 2025) and Israel's equal employment opportunity rules, how long can we keep candidate data after an event for a recruiter follow-up within 90 days? And does a per-hire success fee make us a regulated "placement agency"?
