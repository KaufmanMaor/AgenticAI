# Round 1: VP HR / Head of TA reality check

*Perspective: scaled hiring in Israeli startups and a multinational R&D center, and I've staffed plenty of booths. Prices below are my own estimates from the field, not researched figures.*

## 1. Would I pay? Yes, but not much, and not from the budget you think

- **Budget line: events and employer branding**, where booths and swag sit (₪20K–80K for a booth at a Cyber Week or AI Week-type event). Sourcing tools are locked annual contracts; agency spend is per-requisition and success-based. Nobody moves agency money into an app.
- **What I'd actually sign:** **₪3–5K per event per company** as an add-on to the booth package. Or a **per-held-meeting fee of ₪200–400**, which I like more because I only pay when something happens. If you're promising "pre-matched candidates who said yes to my req", I'd go up to ₪8K for a hot role.
- **Success fee?** I'd only consider it for hard roles, at roughly ₪10–15K per hire (an Israeli agency takes about 1–1.5 monthly salaries, so ₪45–70K for a senior engineer). Be warned that attribution fights will eat you alive: "we already had him in Comeet from 2024." Don't build the business on that.
- The honest framing: **you are an upgrade to lead retrieval, not a replacement for agencies.** Your ROI comparison is "booth ₪40K produced 2 hires, or 0".

## 2. What a recruiter does at a conference today, and what hurts

Today we stand at the booth with a raffle, hoodies and a careers-page QR code, scan badges and collect business cards. Roughly 80% of the people we talk to are students, vendors and swag hunters. Hiring managers promise to come by and give one talk instead.

The pains:
1. **We can't tell who's relevant** until we're 5 minutes into a conversation.
2. **No CV, no consent.** The lead turns into a WhatsApp message three days later. Half are lost and the rest go into an Excel sheet.
3. **The people we want don't walk up to the booth.** A senior engineer doesn't want his manager to see him chatting at a competitor's booth.
4. **ROI can't be measured**, so the booth budget gets cut every year.

**What would make me send a recruiter *because of your app*:** 48 hours before the event, show me "**14 attendees match your 3 open reqs and agreed to meet; 6 slots already booked.**" Guaranteed meetings are worth something to me. "Maybe someone will be there" is worth nothing.

## 3. Fit: where it works and where it fails

| Fits well | Fits badly |
|---|---|
| Mid-level (3–8 yrs) in hot-but-not-impossible roles: DevOps/SRE, QA automation, data engineering, sales engineers, product, customer success | Staff/principal engineers and executives. That's headhunter work, and the discretion bar is too high |
| Sector conferences where the industry is concentrated: cyber, semiconductors, medtech/regulatory, fintech | Juniors at general conferences. The Israeli junior market is flooded, so you'd hand me 200 bootcamp grads and I'd mute the app |
| Returning reservists, olim, people coming back after relocating abroad: high intent, little to hide | Roles needing clearance or unusual work authorization, unless that's captured up front |

Senior people don't "job seek." They are **open to a conversation**. Design for that.

## 4. The "first interview on the spot"

Call it an **intro conversation, not an interview.** If you say "interview", legal will get nervous and candidates will feel exposed.

A realistic version is a **12–15 minute structured screen**: motivation, notice period (30 days is typical, 60–90 for seniors), salary range fit, hybrid/location, two role-specific probe questions, and then **book the next step on the spot**. It is not a technical interview. If that next-step slot isn't in the calendar before the candidate leaves the venue, the whole meeting was worth nothing.

**What I need before I say yes to a meeting:** title, years of experience, the *type* of current company (startup or enterprise, not the name), core skills/stack, notice period, whether the salary band roughly matches, location, and clearance/work authorization where relevant. The LinkedIn profile is revealed only after both sides accept. **No photo before the match.** It isn't needed, and it's where bias starts.

## 5. What happens after

- **One-tap export to the ATS.** In Israel that means Comeet first, then Greenhouse, Workday and SmartRecruiters. Each record carries a source tag ("EventX-2026"), the recruiter's notes, a scorecard and **the consent record.** If it's not in the ATS, it doesn't exist.
- Your "data deleted after the event" promise collides with this. Fix it by **keeping the data only for matches the candidate accepted**, with explicit consent to transfer, and deleting everything else.
- **Hiring manager handoff:** push the candidate and the recruiter's notes to the hiring manager, with their calendar already open for slots.
- **48-hour SLA:** if the company doesn't follow up, the candidate automatically gets a status update. Ghosting is what will kill trust on the candidate side.
- **Funnel analytics:** meetings, then next steps, then offers, then hires per event. That's my renewal argument to the CFO.

## 6. Discretion: "my boss is here"

Israel is one big WhatsApp group. How candidates will actually behave:
- Employed people **will not** toggle "job seeking." Some will toggle a quiet "open to a coffee" setting, **if** it's visible only to verified recruiters.
- **Mandatory controls:** automatically hide the candidate from their current employer's domain, its parent company and its contracted agencies, plus a manual block list. Candidates must also never appear to their *own* company's recruiters, or HR will start checking who's leaving.
- Verify recruiters through a work email and the organizer's badge; expect lurkers.
- **Sponsor conflict:** the company paying ₪150K for the main sponsorship will be furious if a competitor poaches its engineers at its own event. The organizer needs a rule for that, and so does your pitch.

## 7. Legal and ethical (Israel)

- **Equal Employment Opportunities Law (1988)** applies from the screening stage: age, sex, pregnancy and parenthood, religion, nationality, place of residence, and **reserve duty**. Since 2023 reserve-duty discrimination is a real litigation risk. The product must **not allow filtering by these attributes**, and the scorecard should steer recruiters away from asking about miluim or about family.
- Quick in-person screens amplify halo and appearance bias. Use structured questions and notes templates. A note like "too old for our vibe" is discoverable in court.
- **Privacy Protection Law, Amendment 13 (in force since Aug 2025):** job-seeking status is sensitive employment data. You need explicit consent per company, purpose limitation, and real deletion. Get a privacy lawyer before the pilot, not after.

## 8. The remote video booth: gimmick for v1

A computer in a noisy expo hall is neither private nor dignified, and a phone already does Zoom. The real uses are narrow: a multinational with no recruiter on the ground, or **a hiring manager joining by video for 5 minutes right after the screen.** Cut the hardware. Replace it with "**book a quiet-room slot**" plus phone video.

## 9. What the product must do to fit TA workflow

1. Requisition-based: companies post specific open roles, not a general "we're hiring".
2. Pre-event matching (T-48h) with mutual opt-in and **booked slots**, plus a live "here now" on the day.
3. Structured candidate cards (section 4) and team seats for several recruiters plus hiring managers.
4. Calendar sync, a meeting-point map, and a scorecard template.
5. ATS export with consent, a follow-up SLA, and ROI analytics.
6. Discretion controls (section 6) on by default.

## 10. Two bold ideas

**A. Hiring-manager-led, not recruiter-led.** "Coffee with the team lead from Company X." Engineers trust engineers, and team leads already attend the talks. The recruiter runs logistics in the background. I'd bet response rates beat recruiter outreach by a wide margin (to be tested). It also solves "why would I send a recruiter": you don't need to, because the hiring manager is already there.

**B. Passive "Open to a coffee" plus company "office hours".** No "job seeker" label at all. The candidate signals "open to hearing about X-type roles, discreetly." Companies publish 30-minute office-hour slots at the venue ("ask our platform team anything"). That turns the asymmetry into a browsable schedule and gives passive seniors a deniable reason to show up: "I was just asking about their architecture."

## Open questions for the other roles

1. **Organizer/BD:** Will organizers enable a hiring layer that lets non-sponsors poach sponsors' employees? What does a revenue share or sponsor protection have to look like for them to say yes?
2. **Product/UX:** How do we get the ratio of qualified candidates to recruiters right at the venue, so recruiters don't drown in juniors and candidates don't face an empty screen? Gating? Caps? Req-matching thresholds?
3. **Legal/Privacy:** Can "delete after event" and "consented export to the employer's ATS" coexist under Amendment 13 and GDPR? And who is the data controller for meeting notes: us, the organizer or the employer?
